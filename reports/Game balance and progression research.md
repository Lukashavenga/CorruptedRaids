# Lock the level, steepen the loot, keep four stats

Every source agrees on one thing. Corrupted Raids does not need more systems. It needs four of the ones it has to be tightened. **First, lock the fight's level at a visible moment in the gathering window and make turnout pay out rather than hurt.** No shipped game found re-picks its difficulty tier each time someone joins, and the one controlled study of live re-scaling found players did not want enemies changing strength on them. **Second, keep how often loot drops and fix how rare it is.** Live drops run at 0.29 items per viewer per run. The rarity mix is 28% common and 44% rare-or-better, the reverse of every published loot table, and no legendary has ever dropped. **Third, swap 300 invisible +2-point levels for about 50 levels that each show what they unlocked.** WoW and Diablo IV both made this move for the reasons the streamer gives. **Fourth, keep all four stats.** Measured with the real engine, removing Speed takes a typical win rate from 83% to 36%. The real problems are Skill saturating by level 25 and DPS having a fixed Attack/Speed split, which looks like a choice and is not one. For the economy, build merge (#15) before any auction house (#16): Diablo III's gold auction house is the documented warning. And keep gear requirements (#22), because they are already almost gone (113 of 127 items are ungated) and the few left tie levels to gear. The alpha log is small: **45 live fights, 29 viewers, two sessions**. It supports conclusions about roles, loot rates and gold flows. It cannot support per-dungeon win-rate tuning, or anything about Levels 1, 5, 6 or raids.

## The answers

## The alpha log is a playtest, not a verdict: 45 fights across two sessions

**Verified.** The log holds **45 dungeon fights, all tagged `live`**, with 317 member rows from 29 viewers. They were played on 2026-10-28 (27 fights) and 2026-10-29 (18 fights). There are no raid fights, because both raids are `enabled: false`, and there are no `sim` or `sandbox` rows. The median party is 7 and the largest is 14. Each dungeon has **7-11 fights**, and each dungeon-by-level cell has **1-8**. At those sizes a 95% interval on a win rate is roughly **±25-35 percentage points**: 7 wins in 7 fights is consistent with anything from about 65% to 100%. The whole log ran under one balance fingerprint. Every group fight landed on **Level 3 or 4**. Levels 1, 5 and 6 were never fielded, and Level 2 appears only as three solo wipes. Many testers arrived already at levels 30-74, so the log says nothing about a fresh chat's first night ([alpha log](../research_notes/Game%20balance%20and%20progression%20research/alpha_balance_log.md)).

**Inference.** Some findings can be trusted at this size: role death rates, damage shares, the loot rate against its knobs, where gold goes, and the fact that small or healer-less parties lose. Others cannot. Do not retune any single dungeon's `targetWinRate` or `bandStatScale` from this log; wait for about 30 fights per cell. The simulator is still the only evidence for Levels 1, 2, 5 and 6.

## 1. Difficulty: lock the level when people commit, then let turnout pay

### The problem in the live data: one in three fights sat on a level line

**Verified.** Group fights rated between **489 and 1,179**. The Level 3/4 boundary in `BAND_THRESHOLDS` (`brutal: 940` in [squad.ts](../src/engine/squad.ts)) runs through the middle of that range. **16 of 42 group fights (38%) rated within 60 points of 940**, close enough that one join or leave could plausibly have flipped the level. The schema records only the level when the fight started, so how often a late joiner actually moved the level cannot be measured. Small parties are where fights are lost. Parties of four or fewer won **3 of 8**, parties of 5-9 won **21 of 27**, and parties of 10 or more won **8 of 10**. Fights with at least one tank and one healer won **27 of 34 (79%)**, and fights with no healer won **3 of 9**. All three solo fights drew Level 2 and wiped ([alpha log](../research_notes/Game%20balance%20and%20progression%20research/alpha_balance_log.md)).

**Inference.** The log contains a confound. Small parties are also the ones missing a healer, and the strongest testers turned up to the big sessions. "Big parties win" is partly "parties with healers win". The current `partyScaling` (+5% enemy HP per member past five, +65% enemy attack per doubling) is not making large crowds a losing proposition at 5-14 players. The losing case is the small, unbalanced party.

### Shipped games choose the tier before the run and let headcount move only smooth numbers

**Verified.** In every co-op game reviewed, players **pick the difficulty tier before launch**: Deep Rock Galactic's Hazard, Mythic+ keystones, Diablo III's difficulty. Only the headcount multiplier moves, and games differ on when they sample it. WoW's Flexible raids scale "depending on how many players are in the raid at the time the bosses are encountered" ([Warcraft Wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Flexible_Raid)). Diablo II multiplies a monster's HP "when a monster is created", by (players+1)/2, and **raises XP by the same factor** ([Arreat Summit](https://classic.battle.net/diablo2exp/monsters/basics.shtml)). Diablo III's patch 1.0.8 cut the extra monster health per player from 70% to 50%. In the same patch it **added explicit multiplayer XP, Gold Find and Magic Find bonuses** ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2013-04-03-diablo-iii-gets-massive-multiplayer-improvements-in-1-0-8.html)). Blizzard's stated goal for Flex is almost exactly this game's complaint: "you should never feel encouraged to turn away interested players". Blizzard also replaced step breakpoints with weighted randomness so that "adding an extra player will always give you an average of 0.2 extra Bane debuffs. No more breakpoints!" ([Warcraft Wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Flexible_Raid)). Marbles on Stream goes further still: "More players in a race means more positions earn points — bigger lobbies reward more racers" ([Marbles wiki](https://wiki.pixelbypixel.studio/racing)). The audience games reviewed all **close entry before the event resolves**: Marbles takes `!play` only "when registration opens", Stream Raiders has a placement period, and Streamlabs' heist has a start delay with a minimum-entries refund ([Streamlabs](https://streamlabs.com/content-hub/post/how-to-use-modules-in-streamlabs-desktop-cloudbot-101)). No shipped game was found that raises the difficulty *tier* as late players join.

**Verified (research).** Hunicke and Chapman's founding difficulty-adjustment paper prefers "off stage" changes, because "reactive changes run the risk of disrupting the player's sense of disbelief… causing the game to appear schizophrenic" ([Hamlet PDF](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/pubs/Hamlet.pdf)). The only controlled comparison of locked against live adjustment in a multiplayer game is small (**n=23**, in pairs, not counterbalanced). Its players hoped "mob strength didn't change", because a sudden level-up meant "their work was lost" ([Zeng & Sweetser 2022](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/bitstreams/4ae20c15-6ca4-4171-ac7a-ae5393491e0b/download)). Across about 2.2 million level ratings, enjoyment peaks where actual difficulty **matches what players expected** ([Brändle et al. 2025](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12488971/fullTextXML)). Live adjustment improves performance but not measured enjoyment or flow (n=221, [Robb & Zhang 2022](https://actaludologica.com/?p=1527)). Telling players the game adapts does not hurt immersion, and in one study it raised it ([Denisova & Cairns 2019](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/139704/1/1_s2.0_S1875952118300521_main.pdf)).

**Inference.** The banner level is the expectation a viewer commits to. A late joiner who bumps it breaks that expectation for everyone already in, and does so visibly. That is the "schizophrenic" on-stage change the research warns against, and the breakpoint Blizzard removed. The fix that fits "less is more" has three parts: one lock moment, one turnout bonus, and nothing re-rated in hiding. The mean rating stays. It is the safe aggregate for player-versus-environment play. Research on player-versus-player teams finds the *strongest* member predicts results best ([Dehpanah et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11397)), and Apex rates premade teams by their HIGHEST member while admitting it makes it "very difficult for new players to join in with their more skillful friends" ([EA](https://www.ea.com/en-gb/games/apex-legends/news/matchmaking-2023)). That is the failure Corrupted Raids' mean was chosen to avoid.

**Inference on win targets.** No research gives a game-specific optimal win rate. The "85% rule" is a learning-theory result ([Roohi et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.12937), citing Wilson et al. 2019). Engagement data leans toward *easier*, and Brändle finds players motivated by success. On a stream every wipe is public, and a loss already cuts pay: defeat pays 0.15× XP and no gold. Monks, at a 50% target, measured 45% live and paid about half the drops and a third of the gold of the other dungeons. It is working as designed. The question is whether a coin flip is the right design for any dungeon a chat meets.

## 2. Levelling: turn 300 invisible levels into about 50 visible ones

### The curve's shape is fine; its scale and its reward are not

**Verified (repo and log).** The cost of the next level is `40 + 10 × level` (`xpToNextLevel` in `src/engine/stats.ts`), scaled by `progression.xpRate: 0.25`. A level grants `progression.pointsPerLevel: 2`. Live viewers earned about **110 XP per run**, ranging from 52 at poors to 172 at cops and lady-of-knight, and gained about **0.35 levels per run** at levels 30-40. At 110 XP a run, level 300 takes **about 4,186 runs**, not the ~2,920 AGENTS.md budgets. Level 12 takes ~10 runs and level 20 takes ~25 ([progression notes](../research_notes/Game%20balance%20and%20progression%20research/progression_pacing.md)). The starter dungeon pays the least XP, even to high-level parties.

**Verified (precedent).** Pascal Luban names this exact curve shape, where each level's cost rises by a fixed step, "an interesting compromise". He adds that "time spent by the player between two thresholds is as important as the values" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/quantitative-design---how-to-define-xp-thresholds-)). Ian Schreiber's rule is that each step should take "a little bit longer than the last" ([Game Balance Concepts](https://gamebalanceconcepts.wordpress.com/2010/08/18/level-7-advancement-progression-and-pacing/)). WoW's director called levelling to 120 "kind of an empty experience" ([Blizzard Watch](https://blizzardwatch.com/2019/03/22/level-squish-inevitable/)). Shadowlands then cut the cap to 60 and promised that "each and every level will also unlock a new ability, talent, or some other upgrade so that each level is meaningful" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/world-of-warcrafts-level-cap-is-being-reduced-to-60/)). Diablo IV 2.0 split character levels 1-60 from the long Paragon track "with the intent of getting you to endgame activities and gear at a more rapid pace". In that move a level-100 character became level 50 with 200 Paragon levels ([Blizzard](https://news.blizzard.com/diablo4/24130178)). Bungie's verdict on its power-grind season fits ticket #38 closely: "grinding Power will never be a substitute for earning a trophy" ([The Game Post](https://thegamepost.com/bungie-edge-of-fate-was-the-wrong-path-destiny-2-power-tier-grind/)). On motivation, people speed up as a goal nears, about 20% in Kivetz et al.'s café data, and slow down just after a reward ([Kivetz et al. 2006](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/1200/goalgradient.pdf)). A head start on a fresh bar raises completion ([Nunes & Drèze 2006](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v32y2006i4p504-512.html)). Badge-chasers accelerate near the boundary ([Anderson et al. 2013](https://archives.iw3c2.org/www2013/proceedings/p95.pdf)).

**Inference.** A bar that fills several times a session never reaches a visible "nearly there" stretch, which is where the motivation research says effort concentrates. And 300 levels of an invisible +2 is the WoW-120 problem in miniature. Design levels in **runs per level**, the unit chat understands, and derive the XP from that.

### A worked 50-level proposal that keeps the gear gates on schedule

**Inference (our arithmetic).** Set runs per level to `min(25, 3 + 0.5 × (level − 1))` and XP to next level to that figure × 110. With 5 points per level, the catalogue's gates need no retuning:

| Level reached | Runs for that level | Cumulative runs | Points at 5/level | Points today (2/level) at the same run count |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 4.5 | 15 | 20 | 28 |
| 10 | 7 | 45 | 45 | 54 |
| 20 | 12 | 142 | 90 | 102 |
| 30 | 17 | 290 | 145 | 150 |
| 50 (cap) | 25 | 730 | 245 | 244 |

That is about 730 runs: 7 weeks at 100 runs a week, 29 weeks at 25. The first night gives 2-4 level-ups, and later about one a night. **Verified (repo):** the highest gate in the catalogue is **32 points** (`emberbrand.json`, `requires: {atk: 32}`), far below the 245-point cap, so no item becomes unreachable. The proposal is about 30% slower on the first night. A one-off +5 to +10 points at level 1 closes that gap, and it doubles as an endowed head start. The year-long budget in AGENTS.md §2 would not survive. Under "less is more" the long tail belongs to gear and titles, not to a 300-level bar. Existing characters need a conversion rule. The simplest keeps every allocated point and sets the level to the matching point total, following Diablo IV's conversion.

## 3. Loot: keep the frequency, fix the inverted rarity curve

### Live drops are triple what the docs say, and the mix is upside down

**Verified (repo and log).** The live store and `content/balance.json` agree: `survivorLootChance` **0.7088**, `casualtyLootChance` **0.1418**, `defeatLootChance` **0.0709**, then `mobDropChance` **0.6**. A boss fight always passes that second check. The effective chance per non-boss fight is therefore **42.5%** for a survivor, **8.5%** for someone who died in a win, and **4.3%** for anyone in a losing party. The log matches: survivors of wins got **0.42** drops, and all members averaged **0.29 drops per viewer per run**. That is 2.9 per viewer on the 9.8-run first day, against the "0.08-0.15 per viewer per run, about one item a night" AGENTS.md §4 still describes. The order survived > died in a win > lost holds (0.42 / 0.13 / 0.02). The 93 fight drops were **28% common, 28% uncommon, 33% rare, 11% epic and 0% legendary**. The 89 chest openings show the same shape ([alpha log](../research_notes/Game%20balance%20and%20progression%20research/alpha_balance_log.md)).

**Verified (shipped odds).** Every disclosed package has a steep falloff and a floor above the bottom tier. Hearthstone's official odds are a legendary at 5% per pack, "guaranteed to get one within 40 packs", an epic at 21% within 10 packs, and "at least one Normal Rare card or better" in every pack ([Hearthstone wiki, quoting Blizzard](https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Card_pack_statistics)). Per card, that comes to roughly 70% common, 25% rare, 4% epic and 1% legendary. Genshin's official average after pity is 85.4% / 13% / 1.6% ([Genshin wiki](https://genshin-impact.fandom.com/wiki/Character_Event_Wish)). Diablo IV's Season 4 "significantly decreased the number of items that drop… fewer items will drop overall, but those items will be more likely to be valuable" ([Blizzard](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/24077223/)). Diablo III also went "fewer, better" and then **doubled** legendary drops, a change "overwhelmingly popular" with players ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2014-05-22-diablo-3-legendary-drop-rate-increase-now-permanent.html)).

**Verified (research).** Rarer loot produces larger arousal, a larger urge to open more, and longer pauses after the reward ([Larche et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31760581/)). Anticipation tracks uncertainty, which is highest at **P = 0.5** ([Fiorillo et al. 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12649484/)). Hopson warns that "reducing the level of reinforcement is a very punishing thing for your players… Sudden loss of reward is very aversive" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/behavioral-game-design)).

**Inference.** The streamer's history is Hopson's contrast effect playing out: a cut to roughly a quarter on 10-26, then "too stingy" a day later, and two raises since. The per-run roll of about 42% already sits near the uncertainty peak, so changing frequency again gains little and risks a fresh contrast. "Less is more" is reached by making **rare things rare** and adding a real top tier, not by giving out fewer items. With 15 viewers on a 12-run night, a 1% legendary share means the chat sees roughly one legendary every other night. That is close to Diablo III's group target of about one every 90 minutes ([FullCleared](https://fullcleared.com/news/diablo-iii-will-internal-legendary-drop-timer/)).

### Bad-luck protection protects the unlucky only, and counts fights, not time

**Verified.** Shipped pity systems cap the worst streak at about 1.4-2× the average wait. Hearthstone's legendary averages 20 packs and is capped at 40. WoW Legion's protection "exists to protect the unluckiest players", and it had "no time component… If you log off for a month, you aren't more or less likely" ([PCGamesN](https://pcgamesn.com/world-of-warcraft/wow-legion-legendary-soft-cap-removed); [Blizzard Watch](https://blizzardwatch.com/2016/10/14/legion-patch-7-1-developer-qa-liveblog/)). Destiny shows the tier first, as the engram colour, and reveals the item later. It had to patch engrams so that a purple "will always produce a Legendary-quality item or higher" ([Destiny wiki](https://destiny.fandom.com/wiki/Destiny_Update_1.0.2)). Near-misses drive the strongest urge to keep playing ([Larche et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27435416/)). And Overwatch players who *earned* rather than bought loot boxes showed greater gambling-related harms ([Larche et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35834117/)), so free does not mean harmless.

### Recommendations: loot

| Change | Knob / place | Basis |
|---|---|---|

## 4. Economy: the shop is the only sink, so add merge before an auction house

### Gold earned 36.7k, gold spent 22.4k, and 69% of the spending went on one ungated staff

**Verified (log and repo).** Viewers earned **36,703 gold** in fights (about 116 per member-fight) and spent about **22,366**: 61% of what they earned. Of that, **15,444 went on six copies of the Shardspire Staff** at the 2,574g shelf override. It is a legendary with **`requires: {}`** (no gate) that gives atk 10, spd 3 and skill 12. Overrides of 4,200-8,745 have bought nothing; at ~116g a run they cost 36-75 runs. Found items recycle at `lootRecycleRate: 0.1`: 57 items returned **536g**, about 9g each ([alpha log](../research_notes/Game%20balance%20and%20progression%20research/alpha_balance_log.md)). **Shop refunds are smaller than the docs claim.** `boughtRecycleOf` pays `value × recycleRate`, not the shelf price ([shopPricing.ts](../src/engine/shopPricing.ts)). So a 2,574g staff with a legendary `value` of 600 refunds **600g (23%)**, and each buy-and-recycle destroys 1,974g ([item economy notes](../research_notes/Game%20balance%20and%20progression%20research/item_economy.md)).

**Verified (theory and live economies).** Ultima Online's postmortem is where faucets and sinks began. Selling to NPC shops "is not a true sink of goods because it involves printing gold", and consumables are "by far the most politically feasible drain because the players feel they are getting something" ([Simpson 1999](https://gwern.net/doc/economics/1999-04-07-simpson-theingameconomicsofultimaonline.html)). Lehdonvirta and Castronova warn that a publisher shop used as the only money sink "is also simultaneously a faucet for items". They report that random-output recyclers, where the better the output the lower its odds, "are the most efficient in getting users to dispose of their items" ([Virtual Economies, ch. 11](https://dokumen.pub/virtual-economies-design-and-analysis-information-policy-reprintnbsped-9780262535069-0262535068-0262027259-9780262027250.html)). Dormans: two converters in a loop form an engine that "needs to be balanced by applying some sort of friction" ([Dormans 2012](https://eprints.illc.uva.nl/id/eprint/2118/1/DS-2012-12.text.pdf)). In August 2026 EVE's single largest sink was the **transaction tax (29.6T ISK)**, yet total sinks removed only about 58% of what faucets added ([CCP](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/monthly-economic-report-august-2026)). Consolation value for an unwanted drop in shipped games clusters at **12.5-25% of the item's cost**: Hearthstone's disenchant runs 5/40 to 400/1,600 ([Hearthstone wiki](https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Crafting)), and Overwatch's duplicates refunded 20% ([Overwatch wiki](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Credits)). In November 2025 Supercell *removed* two upgrade currencies from Clash Royale because they "felt a bit confusing", and with them players "were spending more energy managing their inventory than having fun" ([Supercell](https://supercell.com/en/games/clashroyale/blog/news/coming-in-the-next-update)).

**Verified (auction houses).** Diablo III's director said at GDC 2013 that both auction houses "really hurt the game". Over 50% of players used them regularly, and "gold does much more damage than the other one does" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2013-03-28-diablo-3-director-jay-wilson-auction-houses-really-hurt-game.html)). This was despite a **flat 15% fee** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diablo_III)). Blizzard's removal post: the auction house "ultimately undermines Diablo's core game play: kill monsters to get cool loot" ([Blizzard](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/10974978/diablo-iii-auction-house-update)). Path of Exile's Trade Manifesto argues easy trade means "a much higher acquisition rate of useful items", so the game must either cut drops or get easier, and that "we had to be careful not to make it too easy" ([GGG](https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/2025870)).

**Inference.** Corrupted Raids has three gold faucets: fight gold, `startingGold` and recycling. It has one real gold sink, the shop, which is also an item faucet, plus consumables. Merge (#15) would be the first **pure item sink**, and a small merge fee the first gold sink with no item attached. The staff shows players *will* save for one visible, aspirational item. But an ungated legendary on the shelf is also the shortest path to power, and it plausibly feeds the attack-heavy healer builds in §5. The Diablo III record says the damage came from how central the auction house was, not from its fee. With 29 live viewers the market would be thin anyway, which makes #16 low value now.

## 5. Stats: keep four, fix Skill saturation and the DPS false choice

### What the engine and the live builds say

**Verified (engine probes).** These were run against the real engine with the `report:threat` reference party (2 tanks, 2 healers, 8 DPS), **n=300 fights per cell**, where differences under ~6 points are noise ([stat review notes](../research_notes/Game%20balance%20and%20progression%20research/stat_system_review.md)). **Removing Speed** dropped the win rate in the fight the party is actually matched to from **83% to 36%** at Level 10 in typical gear, and from **47% to 12%** at Level 25 in best gear. Folding Attack and Speed into one "Might" stat stayed within noise (81% and 58% in the same fight), but needs re-measured thresholds, a full re-solve of every dungeon, and edits to about 70 gear files. Merging HP and Skill leaves the Tank a one-stat role. A forced symmetric three-stat model makes DPS want HP and loses (58% / 40%). DPS need Attack *and* Speed together: all-Attack wins 61% and all-Speed 43%, against 83% for the split. **Skill drives seven mechanics** and caps between Skill 20 and 50: mitigation at 30 (`combat.maxMitigation` 0.75, `skillCurveK` 10), tank self-mitigation at 33, party guard at 35, tank threat at 50, and healer bonus speed at 20 (`roles.healer.maxSpdBonus` 3, `spdPerSkill` 0.15). **Skill appears on 112 of 127 gear files**, so a Level-25 DPS has Skill 33 with zero points spent, already at the mitigation cap. `ratePoints` is role-blind. A DPS who buys HP *lowers* the party's rating, draws an easier fight and wins more (67-85% in that fight, against 48-65% in the same fight), which is a hole chat could find.

**Verified (live builds).** DPS die at **45%**, against 21% for tanks and 18% for healers. Tanks take 66% of hits. Tanks fill 33% of slots against an ideal 17%. Five veteran healer characters (average level 62) dealt **34% of all damage** and put more points into **Attack** than into any other stat. Per point of Corruption, though, DPS still out-damage them. **13 of 29 viewers had unspent points**, and on average viewers had allocated about 0.67 of the 2 × level points available ([alpha log](../research_notes/Game%20balance%20and%20progression%20research/alpha_balance_log.md)). This is five characters and small cells, so read the healer pattern as a lead, not a verdict.

**Verified (precedent).** Shipped games cut stats that are **non-choices**, not stats that are merely weak. WoW removed hit and expertise because "Most players treated Hit/Expertise caps as mandatory… a trap of sorts". In the same patch notes it kept Warrior Haste and changed the class "to ensure that Haste has strong, competitive value". It stated the role-split rule directly: "all secondary stats are valuable (except Bonus Armor for non-tanks and Spirit for non-healers, of course)" ([Blizzplanet, WoD alpha notes](https://warcraft.blizzplanet.com/?p=39875)). Diablo III's one-main-stat change was about item legibility, making it "easy to identify other items as not for your character" ([GameBanshee](https://www.gamebanshee.com/ku6xk)). Destiny 2 fixed the stat everyone maxed at a baseline for all players ([BWU archive of Bungie TWID](https://destiny.bungie.org/bwu/559)). A "false choice" is two stats that one role "clearly needs both" of ([Kitsune Games](https://kitsunegames.com/post/archived/2013/06/02/midboss-stats-and-you/)). Sirlin's term for options like that is "complexity that makes the game harder to learn yet no more interesting to play" ([Sirlin](https://www.sirlin.net/articles/balancing-multiplayer-games-part-1-definitions)).

**Inference.** Four stats is the smallest set that keeps "each role wants two, each stat is wanted by two roles" *and* lets gear write trade-offs such as "+Attack, −Speed", which 9 items do. The streamer's "less is more" is served better by **presenting** the stats more simply than by merging them. What each role should spend on:

| Role | Spend on | One-line explanation for chat |
|---|---|---|
| Tank | Health + Skill | Health is how much you can take. Skill is how little each hit does, how hard you pull enemies off your friends, and how much you shield the party. Skill first until ~35, then Health. |
| Healer | Skill + Speed | Skill is how big each heal is, and it makes you act a little more often. Speed is how often you act at all. When nobody needs healing you attack. |
| DPS | Attack + Speed, evenly | Damage is attack × turns, so pumping one does little. Gear gives you Skill. Keep a little Health: about one hit in five ignores the tank. |

|---|---|---|

## 6. Audience design: show each viewer what they did

**Verified (research).** The audience-participation-game literature is small and qualitative: 35 participants in Seering et al., 2-5 playtesters per test in Glickman et al. It is consistent, though. "Audience participants must be able to understand the game situation, how they might influence it, and how their actions affected the outcome", and "mechanics that show clear evidence of an individual's contribution will be more likely to capture that individual's interest" ([Seering et al. 2017](https://joseph.seering.org/papers/Seering_etal_2017_Audience_Participation_Games.pdf)). The closest analogue to Corrupted Raids, an idle role-based Twitch game, failed because "players also had trouble understanding the impact of different roles… and the effects of game events" ([Striner et al. 2021](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/30827/30827.pdf)). Glickman et al. state the core trade-off: as players and their inputs multiply, "the ability for players to make sense of the game as a system decreases". They also describe a "Counterstrike strategy", in which eliminated players become spectators and the endgame grows easier to follow ([Glickman et al. 2018](https://joseph.seering.org/papers/Glickman_etal_2018_Design_Challenges.pdf)). In Seering's playtests, votes totalled every 30 seconds behind a variable stream delay left viewers unable to tell which cycle their vote counted in, "directly reducing feelings of individual agency". Viewers of channels under 500 are the most socially motivated ([Hilvert-Bruce et al. 2018](https://researchprofiles.canberra.edu.au/en/publications/social-motivations-of-live-streaming-viewer-engagement-on-twitch/)). Leaderboards motivate some people and demotivate others (286 participants, [Jia et al. 2017](https://stephen.voida.com/uploads/Publications/Publications/jia-chi17.pdf)). Only about **20%** of users respond to badges ([Hoernle et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06160)). Temporal landmarks such as a new day prompt fresh effort ([Dai et al. 2014](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Dai_Fresh_Start_2014_Mgmt_Sci.pdf)). Marbles on Stream caps farming with 10-minute timers, at most three active ([Marbles wiki](https://wiki.pixelbypixel.studio/racing)). Riot's clarity rules are: convey gameplay clearly, "always preserve hierarchy", and keep noise minimal, with big-effect moments allowed to be loud ([Riot](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/dev-clarity-in-league)). Working memory holds "about four chunks" ([Cowan 2001](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/44023F1147D4A1D44BDC0AD226838496/S0140525X01003922a.pdf/the-magical-number-4-in-short-term-memory-a-reconsideration-of-mental-storage-capacity.pdf)). Stream Raiders pays its participants "for a win or loss", with a salvage chest on a loss ([Stream Raiders wiki](https://streamraiders.fandom.com/wiki/FAQ)). That backs the casualty and defeat pay Corrupted Raids already has.

**Inference.** The auto-resolved fight already solves the stream-delay problem the way Glickman's teams did: viewers commit first and then watch. What is missing is *attribution*. A roster that floats whoever was just hit to the top (#36) is a direct answer to "show clear evidence of an individual's contribution". It also sinks the dead, which gives the Counterstrike-style shrinking, easier-to-read endgame for free. "Dungeoneer of the day" (#37) should be one named title, never a ranked list with someone at the bottom. Expect it to move about one viewer in five. Base it on a rate with a minimum number of runs, not raw attendance, so the heaviest farmer does not own it.

## How to measure it

Use the repo's own tools and change one thing at a time. **`npm run simulate`** checks that the engine is correct and fails the build. It asserts the loot order survived > died in a win > lost and the shop-price rules, so extend it to assert that no merge, recycle and shop cycle returns more than it costs. **`npm run simulate:progression`** prints the design numbers and asserts nothing. Section 2 gives drops per viewer per run and per night, section 11 gives the turnout curve under `partyScaling`, and the band sections run 25 trials. Run it after any change to `balance.json`, loot tables or formations. **`npm run report:threat -- --set path=value`** measures a balance change without editing the file, for example `--set roles.healer.maxSpdBonus=6` for the Skill caps or `--set rewards.survivorLootChance=0.6`. It reports per-role deaths, hits and heals. **`npm run report:balance`** reads the live log (filters `--from --to --dungeon --source`). It needs the Supabase service key in `.env`. Without one it silently reads `data/logs`, and this research found "0 fights… Nothing to report", which is why the alpha numbers here came from SQL. **`npm run check:formations:live`** checks that every level grows on the one below, against the store the game actually plays. For live data, wait for **~30 fights per dungeon-by-level cell** before calling a win rate off-target. At today's 7-11 fights per dungeon the interval is ±25-35 points.

## Conclusion

Most of the tickets turn out to be the same fix seen from different sides: every system should be legible at the moment a viewer acts on it. Locking the level (#39) protects the expectation a viewer commits to. Visible levels (#38) and visible gates (#22) make the bar mean something. A steep rarity curve makes a drop worth announcing. The recent-hit roster (#36) and a single daily title (#37) give each viewer evidence of their own contribution. Most of these are presentation and rule changes on top of systems the engine already has. They are not new systems, which is "less is more" as the evidence actually supports it.

The cautions all point at changes that are hard to undo. An auction house, a three-stat merge, and another sharp loot cut each have a shipped precedent that went badly or had to be walked back. The cheapest high-value move is also the most boring one. Make the docs and the Loot tab say what the live store actually does, then add the logging fields (level at first join, a pity counter) that will let the next alpha settle the questions 45 fights cannot.
