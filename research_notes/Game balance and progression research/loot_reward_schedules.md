## 1. Reinforcement schedules, motivation, and what makes a drop feel good vs. noise

### Takeaway
Variable-ratio rewards produce the highest, steadiest engagement, and dopamine "anticipation" activity peaks when the outcome is most uncertain (p ~ 0.5) and when the reward is rare/valuable — but frequency and magnitude trade off: research and shipped games both point to "rarer and more meaningful" beating "frequent and trivial", with the arousal tied to the *rarity tier* of what is revealed, not to the count of items.

### Cited Findings
- **[Opinion/applied behaviourism]** John Hopson's "Behavioral Game Design" (Gamasutra, 2001; Hopson later led user research at Bungie) contrasts schedules: under a **fixed ratio** players show a distinct "post-reinforcement pause" right after each reward, which "can be a problem for designers" because the pause is where players quit; under a **variable ratio** "there is always a reason to be active", producing higher, steadier activity. — [Behavioral Game Design (Game Developer)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/behavioral-game-design); attribution and date via [Gameranx](https://gameranx.com/?p=24625)
- **[Research]** A 2014 International Journal of Serious Games study found "the variable-ratio schedule was better in the outcome measures than the fixed-ratio schedule." — [IJSG, Oct 2014 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/764b0a333c784a75ab006371c560e178)
- **[Research]** Counterpoint: a Princeton technical report (Kao et al., 2012, on reward preference in games) notes that variable-ratio schedules "may not be preferred in the context of a video game" — higher response rates are not the same as player preference/enjoyment. — [Princeton TR 2012](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/techreports/2012/931.pdf); [Kao 2012 reward preference (MIT)](https://people.csail.mit.edu/dkao/pdf/kao2012rewardpreference.pdf)
- **[Research]** A University of Tasmania thesis on ratio reinforcement in video gaming reports variable-ratio schedules "have been shown to produce higher rates of responding than fixed ratio schedules." — [Thomas thesis, UTAS](https://figshare.utas.edu.au/articles/thesis/The_influence_of_ratio-reinforcement_on_video-gaming_behaviour/23239106/1)
- **[Research]** Fiorillo, Tobler & Schultz, *Science* 2003 (299:1898-1902): dopamine neurons carry two signals. The **phasic** response at the cue scales monotonically with reward probability (the classic reward-prediction-error signal: a surprising reward fires more than an expected one). A separate **sustained** response — "a gradual increase in activity until the potential time of reward" — tracks **uncertainty**, "being maximal at P = 0.5 and decreasing at higher and lower probabilities." The authors suggest a role in "attention-based learning and risk-taking behavior." — [PubMed 12649484](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12649484/); commentary [Nature Reviews Neuroscience](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn1127); follow-up debate [Niv et al. 2005 (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1182345/)
- **[Research]** Larche, Chini, Lee, Dixon & Fernandes, *Journal of Gambling Studies* 2019 (doi 10.1007/s10899-019-09913-5): loot boxes "containing rarer items are more valuable, arousing, rewarding and urge-inducing"; rarer rewards "trigger larger Skin Conductance Responses and larger force responses"; and there was "an increase in anticipatory arousal prior to the reveal." — [UWSpace full text](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/f3df3e42-ecb1-4cf9-89cf-87626dbfbaf1/full); [Springer DOI](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10899-019-09913-5). (Year cited variously as 2019 online / 2021 vol. 37.)
- **[Research]** Larche, Musielak & Dixon (2017), 60 Candy Crush players: near-misses were more arousing than losses (heart rate, subjective arousal) and "triggered the most substantial urge to continue play" of any outcome. — [UWSpace](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/handle/10012/12522). A later review reportedly failed to reproduce near-miss effects on persistence; not verified here.
- **[Research]** Nick Yee's motivation model (DiGRA 2005; *CyberPsychology & Behavior* 2006): 10 subcomponents in 3 components — **Achievement** (Advancement, Mechanics, Competition), Social, Immersion. Advancement-motivated players "derive satisfaction from reaching goals, leveling quickly and accumulating in-game resources"; Mechanics-motivated players enjoy "analyzing and understanding the underlying numerical mechanics." — [Yee 2006 PDF](https://eclass.uniwa.gr/modules/document/file.php/EEE195/2023-2024/Online%20gaming%20addiction/yee%202006.pdf); [DiGRA 2005](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/view/239); [Yee, Achievement component](https://nickyee.com/daedalus/archives/print/001300.php)
- **[Dev/Opinion]** Diablo IV Season 4 "Loot Reborn" (May 14 2024) explicitly cut quantity for quality: the goal was players spending more time fighting "rather than sorting through the many items"; monsters drop less loot but of higher quality; rares rolled with 2 affixes and legendaries with 3 (fewer, more legible affixes). Changes applied to the Eternal realm too, i.e. Blizzard treated it as a permanent fix, not a seasonal experiment. — [GameSpot](https://Gamespot.com/articles/how-diablo-4-loot-reborn-update-will-make-its-items-and-players-more-powerful-than-ever/1100-6523061/); [Mein-MMO patch notes: "less loot, but stronger"](https://mein-mmo.de/en/diablo-4-season-4-patch-notes-show-what-gets-better-less-loot-but-stronger,1121161/); [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/diablo-4-campfire-chat-loot-reborn)
- **[Opinion]** Player reception: "Imagine if it had been like this since release" — Season 4 praised because players "can simply play it". — [Mein-MMO](https://mein-mmo.de/en/diablo-4-imagine-if-it-had-been-like-this-since-release-season-4-excites-players-because-they-can-simply-play-it,1125922/)

### Inferences
- The "feel" of a drop is driven by **surprise × magnitude**, not by count. A drop that happens ~43% of the time to every survivor is close to the p = 0.5 point where anticipation (sustained dopamine/uncertainty) peaks for *whether* something drops — good for tension — but if most of those drops are common greys, the *magnitude* half is near zero and drops become noise. The lever with the most feel per item is rarity variance, not drop frequency.
- The streamer's oscillation (stingy at ~1/night, back up to frequent) matches the two failure modes: at ~0.1 drops/run the uncertainty signal is near p = 0.1 (low anticipation, feels like "nothing happens"); at very high rates with flat rarity, it is a fixed-ish ratio with trivial magnitude (inventory sorting, Diablo IV's pre-S4 complaint). A middle frequency with a meaningful rarity spread is what both D3 Loot 2.0 and D4 Loot Reborn converged on.
- In a shared Twitch fight, *someone* in a party of 10-20 rolling a rare is effectively observed by everyone, so the vicarious/social version of the variable-ratio hit happens far more often than any individual's. This supports keeping individual rare rates low while making rares loud on the overlay.
- Hopson's post-reinforcement-pause point argues against any reward that is perfectly predictable per run (e.g. "every survivor gets exactly one item"), because the pause after a guaranteed item is a natural quit point; a guaranteed *participation* reward is fine if the exciting item stays variable.

- Could not retrieve Schultz's 1997 *Science* RPE paper or a direct quantitative study of "frequency vs. magnitude" in game loot satisfaction; no peer-reviewed number for an "ideal" drop frequency was found.
- Hopson's article full text could not be fetched (host blocked); claims are from search excerpts.
- No source found that measures Bartle types against loot preference specifically.

## 2. Rarity distributions in practice and the "fewer, better drops" fixes

### Takeaway
Published/derived rarity curves cluster around a steep geometric falloff: roughly 70% common / 23% rare / 4-5% epic / ~1% legendary per item (Hearthstone), with a floor guaranteeing at least one non-common per "package" and top-tier rates of ~1 in 13-20 packages (Overwatch ~7.4% legendary per box; Hearthstone ~1 legendary per 20 packs). Diablo III and IV both fixed "loot explosion" by cutting volume, raising quality and targeting drops to the finder ("smart loot").

### Cited Findings
- **[Dev — official China disclosure, 2017]** Hearthstone: at least 1 Rare or better in every pack; Epic on average 1 per 5 packs; Legendary on average 1 per 20 packs. Korean disclosure later matched. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/blizzards-chinese-hearthstone-website-confirms-card-rarity-drop-rates); [Inven Global (Korea same as China)](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/2407/blizzard-reveals-their-item-rarity-odds-in-korea-to-be-same-as-china)
- **[Community — simulation, not official]** Per-card Hearthstone rarity: Common 71.59%, Rare 22.83%, Epic 4.48%, Legendary 1.10% (500,000 simulated cards); the poster notes the model "still [doesn't] match up statistically with real values." — [Hearthstone wiki talk page](https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Talk:Card_pack_statistics)
- **[Dev — official China disclosure, May 2017]** Overwatch loot boxes: legendary ~7.4% per box (~1 per 13.5 boxes), epic ~18.2% per box (~1 per 5.5), every box guaranteed at least one rare. — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/overwatch/overwatch-loot-legendary-skin-chance/page/0/1); [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2017/05/05/blizzard-reveals-overwatch-loot-box-probabilities-for-china.aspx); [Blizzard Watch](https://blizzardwatch.com/2017/05/05/overwatch-hearthstone-heroes-loot-box-drop-rates-revealed-china/). Fan wiki gives ~7.5%. — [Overwatch wiki](https://breezewiki.discard.no/overwatch/wiki/Loot_Box)
- **[Dev/Community]** Diablo III Loot 2.0 (patch 2.0.1, before Reaper of Souls): players see "far fewer items", rares "especially getting stronger stats"; less white-item clutter, more higher-quality rares. **Smart Drop** tailors a *chance* of drops to the finder's class ("only a chance of an item is a smart drop", so other-character farming still works); it "increases chances that a newly found item can be a potential upgrade." — [Diablo Wiki (fandom)](https://diablo.fandom.com/wiki/Loot_2.0); [PCInvasion](https://www.pcinvasion.com/?p=76681); [Shacknews patch 2.0.1](https://www.shacknews.com/article/83266/diablo-3-patch-201-deploys-with-loot-20); [PureDiablo Smart Loot](https://www.purediablo.com/diablo4/Smart_Loot). Release date conflict: Shacknews excerpt said March 2013, but [GameWatcher](https://www.gamewatcher.com/2014-25-02-diablo-iii-s-long-awaited-patch-2-0-1-live-today-features-loot-2-0-system) dates 2.0.1 live on 25 Feb 2014 (the latter is consistent with Reaper of Souls' March 2014 launch).
- **[Dev]** Diablo III lead designer Travis Day stated a target of roughly **one legendary per ~90 minutes of farming**, and that legendaries would roll base stats at the level of the dropping monster. — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/diablo/diablo-3-developers-plan-make-legendaries-more-powerful); [Engadget 2013 itemization plans](https://www.engadget.com/2013-03-05-diablo-iii-plans-itemization-changes.html)
- **[Dev]** In May 2014 the anniversary buff (effectively **doubling** legendary drop rate) was made permanent "due to the overwhelming popularity" — i.e. even after "fewer better", the top tier was tuned *up* when players felt starved. — [MP1st](https://mp1st.com/news/diablo-3s-increased-legendary-drop-rate-stay-says-blizzard); [Gameranx](https://gameranx.com/updates/id/22069/article/blizzard-gives-fans-what-they-want-doubles-legendary-drop-rate-in-diablo-3/); [GameWatcher](https://www.gamewatcher.com/2014-22-05-diablo-iii-to-retain-bonus-buff-after-anniversary-event)
- **[Dev]** Diablo IV S4: fewer affixes per item (rare 2, legendary 3), item power gated by world tier (Sacred only in WT3+, only Ancestral in WT4) — difficulty gates the quality floor. — [Mein-MMO](https://mein-mmo.de/en/diablo-4-season-4-patch-notes-show-what-gets-better-less-loot-but-stronger,1121161/); [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/gaming/all-big-changes-and-updates-coming-in-diablo-4-season-4-loot-reborn)

### Inferences
- A defensible starting curve for Corrupted Raids' five tiers, borrowed from the Hearthstone/Overwatch shape: per item roughly **common 60-70% / uncommon 20-25% / rare 8-12% / epic 2-4% / legendary 0.5-1%**, varied by dungeon level (D4-style: the quality floor rises with difficulty, rather than the drop count rising).
- The "starter dungeon drops only commons" finding (AGENTS.md §10: 109 drops over 40 runs, 100% common, 5 of 34 commons reachable) violates the one rule every disclosed system shares: a **guaranteed floor above the bottom tier per package** (Hearthstone: 1 rare+/pack; Overwatch: 1 rare+/box). Even the starter fight should be able to drop an uncommon, so the first night's chests are not uniformly grey.
- The D3 Day target (~1 legendary per 90 minutes) maps to a stream: if a night is ~12 runs over ~2-3 hours, "each viewer sees a legendary-tier item for themselves roughly once every 1-3 nights, and the chat sees one most nights" is the analogous cadence.
- "Fewer but better" was paired, both times, with **targeting** (smart loot / class-appropriate drops). Corrupted Raids' analogue is biasing gear rolls toward items the viewer's attributes can actually wear (`requires`), since a drop the player cannot equip is noise however rare.

- No verified numbers retrieved for Diablo II treasure-class odds, Borderlands rarity rates, Path of Exile, WoW raid drop rates, or Destiny engram odds (wikis for these were not reached); these should not be quoted without a source.
- Could not confirm D3's actual legendary drop percentages per kill (only the 90-minute design target).

## 3. Pity timers / bad-luck protection, deterministic vs random, and perceived probability

### Takeaway
Shipped games pair a low base rate with a hard ceiling (Hearthstone 40 packs for a legendary at ~5%/pack average; Genshin 90 pulls with "soft pity" ramping from ~74) or a hidden rising chance (WoW Legion legendaries). Players systematically misjudge probability (Sid Meier: a 3:1 favourite that loses "feels" broken), so the variance the math allows is often variance players read as unfairness; pity caps the worst streak without removing surprise.

### Cited Findings
- **[Dev/Community]** Hearthstone regular legendary: ~5% per pack average, **pity at 40 packs**; golden legendary pity 361 packs; other rarity ceilings in the table (e.g. 10 packs for epic). Each expansion tracks its own counter, so one can still go 60+ packs without a legendary across sets. New sets guarantee at least one legendary within the first 10 packs. — [Hearthstone wiki: Card pack statistics / Pity Timer](https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Pity_Timer); [Esports.gg (cites Blizzard support)](https://esports.gg/news/hearthstone/hearthstone-pity-timer/); [Hearthstone wiki: Card packs](https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Card_packs). Note: older community posts called the 40-pack timer "unconfirmed... more or less proven with aggregate data" — [Reddit analysis mirror](https://reddit.rtrace.io/r/hearthstone/comments/3zaeou/pity_timer_on_packs_opening_analysiskinda_proofed).
- **[Dev]** Hearthstone also has **duplicate protection** (won't give a card you already own the max of) — [Hearthstone wiki: Duplicate protection](https://hearthstone.gamepedia.com/Duplicate_protection)
- **[Community]** Genshin Impact character banner: 5-star base rate ~0.6% per pull; **soft pity** begins ~pull 74 (sources say 73-75), odds rise steeply after; **hard pity** at 90 (90th guaranteed after 89 misses). Weapon banner: soft pity ~65, hard 80. Mechanics are community-derived from logs, not fully official. — [Genshin wiki: Wish](https://genshin-impact.fandom.com/wiki/Wish); [Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/esports/how-pity-work-genshin-impact-soft-pity-50-50-explained); [AFK Gaming](https://afkgaming.com/gaming/genshin-impact/what-is-pity-in-genshin-impact-and-how-does-it-work)
- **[Dev]** WoW Legion legendaries: Blizzard confirmed bad-luck protection that "raises drop chances each time you don't receive a Legendary"; originally it stopped applying once a player had 4 legendaries ("soft cap"), later removed. Sources conflict on whether time played matters (Hazzikostas at the 7.1 Q&A: "there's no time component... it is mostly random"). — [PCGamesN soft cap removed](https://pcgamesn.com/world-of-warcraft/wow-legion-legendary-soft-cap-removed); [Mein-MMO](https://mein-mmo.de/wow-legion-bad-luck-protection-legendary/); [Blizzard Watch 7.1 Q&A liveblog](https://blizzardwatch.com/2016/10/14/legion-patch-7-1-developer-qa-liveblog/)
- **[Dev]** Earlier WoW precedent: Garrosh heirlooms (2014) had bad-luck protection. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2014-04-10-garrosh-heirlooms-have-bad-luck-protection.html)
- **[Community/press]** Diablo III was reported to have an internal legendary drop timer (bad-luck protection) — [FullCleared](https://fullcleared.com/news/diablo-iii-will-internal-legendary-drop-timer/) (page not fetchable; detail unverified).
- **[Dev talk, secondhand]** Sid Meier, GDC 2010 keynote "The Psychology of Game Design (Everything You Know Is Wrong)": playtesters expected to win 3:1 encounters though they lost 25% of the time; in *Civilization Revolution*, which showed odds, players got angry losing at 3:1 and explaining probability did not help; testers showed gambler's-fallacy and hot-hand effects. Meier's response was to bend the math toward perception. — [Aalborg Univ. "Probability in Video Games"](https://projekter.aau.dk/projekter/files/202105490/Probability_in_Video_Games.pdf); [GameBusiness.jp report (Japanese)](https://www.gamebusiness.jp/article/2010/03/13/1289.html); [MCV keynote announcement](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/sid-meier-to-keynote-gdc-2010/); [Strong Museum "Game Psychology 101"](https://www.museumofplay.org/blog/gdc-2010-game-psychology-101/)
- **[Opinion]** Player sentiment shows the perceived-unfairness problem directly: "35 packs opened and not a single legendary" — [Blizzard forums](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/hearthstone/t/35-packs-opened-and-not-a-single-legendary/5297)

### Inferences
- Concrete numbers for Corrupted Raids: with ~43% item chance per surviving run and, say, a 3% epic+ rate per item, P(epic+ per run) ≈ 1.3%; over a 12-run night that is ~15% per viewer, so ~85% of viewers end a given night without one and a few will go 10+ nights. A **per-viewer counter** (e.g. epic-or-better guaranteed within N chests, or chance rising each chest without one, WoW-style) caps the worst streak at a known length; Hearthstone's ratio (pity at 40 vs. mean 20, i.e. ~2x the mean) is a reasonable template. The roster already persists per character, so a counter is cheap (it is state, so it would need a schema field and must survive `parseRoster`).
- Prefer a **soft pity** (rising chance) over a hard counter for the design premise "simple and legible": it removes long droughts while keeping every chest uncertain; a hard guarantee can be shown on the loadout as a visible progress mark ("next chest: better odds") — Hearthstone's new-set "legendary in first 10 packs" shows a front-loaded guarantee is also an option for a viewer's very first night.
- Sid Meier's finding argues for **streak-breaking on the bad side only**: players do not complain about luck that favours them, so pity should cap droughts and never punish a hot streak (WoW's soft cap that stopped protection for lucky players was later removed).
- The current loss/casualty chances (4.3% and 8.5% effective) mean a viewer who dies often can go many nights with no chest; this is where pity/participation counters matter most.

### Gaps
- No official Blizzard support page retrieved for the 40-pack figure (cited via esports.gg); Genshin numbers are community-derived.
- No study found that measures retention effect of pity timers directly.
- "Unrolling streaks" / shuffle-bag ("deck of cards") randomness as used in games: no citable source retrieved in this pass.

## 4. Sealed chests / delayed reveal, anticipation, and loot-box ethics to avoid

### Takeaway
Anticipation itself carries value (Loewenstein's savoring; Larche's measured rise in arousal *before* a reveal), so the sealed-chest-opened-later pattern is psychologically sound — but the same reveal mechanics are what the loot-box literature links to problem gambling (Zendle & Cairns, η² = 0.054 for spending vs. 0.004 for other purchases). With no real money involved the financial harm disappears, but the "predatory" design tactics King & Delfabbro list (hidden odds, manipulated outcomes, pressure, sunk-cost entrapment, near-miss staging) are worth avoiding anyway.

### Cited Findings
- **[Research]** Loewenstein (1987), *Economic Journal* 97(387):666-684: utility from **savoring** (anticipating future consumption) and **dread**; in the illustrative study people would pay more for a kiss from a movie star of their choice three days out than for one immediately. — [CMU PDF](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/AnticipationDelayed.pdf); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ecj/econjl/v97y1987i387p666-84.html). Later work (Kumar, Killingsworth & Gilovich, "Waiting for Merlot") finds anticipation of experiences is more pleasurable than of material goods. — [Kumar et al. PDF](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/blogs.cornell.edu/dist/b/6819/files/2019/12/KumarKillingswothGilovich.14.pdf). Caveat: a non-academic source claims the kiss result was not significant and did not replicate — unverified.
- **[Research]** Larche et al. (2019): measurable **anticipatory arousal prior to the reveal**, and rarer contents produce larger arousal and a greater urge to open more boxes. — [UWSpace](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/f3df3e42-ecb1-4cf9-89cf-87626dbfbaf1/full)
- **[Research]** Zendle & Cairns (2018), *PLOS ONE* 13(11): e0206767, n = 7,422 gamers: loot box spending linked to problem gambling severity (η² = 0.054); other real-money in-game purchases much weaker (η² = 0.004), suggesting the gambling-like features specifically matter. Direction of causation could not be determined ("both directions of causality" may be true). A 2019 replication (n = 1,172) found the link again. — [White Rose PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/139116/1/journal.pone.0206767.pdf); [Univ. of York press release](https://www.york.ac.uk/news-and-events/news/2018/research/researchers-find-link-problem-gambling-loot-boxes); [PsyPost on two studies](https://www.psypost.org/two-large-studies-have-found-a-link-between-loot-box-spending-and-problem-gambling/)
- **[Research]** Zendle later found collectible-card booster packs may *not* be linked to problem gambling in the same way as loot boxes. — reported in [York St John repository](https://ray.yorksj.ac.uk/id/eprint/3619/) / [Zendle & Cairns written evidence to UK Parliament](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/98535/html/)
- **[Research — editorial]** King & Delfabbro (2018), *Addiction* 113:1967-1969, "Predatory monetization schemes in video games (e.g. 'loot boxes') and internet gaming disorder": tactics include "limited disclosure of the product, unavoidable solicitations, and manipulation of reward outcomes", time-limited offers, and psychological "entrapment" (escalating spend because players feel they have invested too much to quit). Random, skill-free outcomes likened to scratch tickets/slots. — [Flinders Research Now](https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/predatory-monetization-schemes-in-video-games-eg-loot-boxes-and-i/); [Univ. of Adelaide news](https://adelaide.edu.au/news/news100942.html); [Durham repository](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1263775)
- **[Research]** Xiao et al. (2019, *Int. J. Mental Health & Addiction*) critique: random reward mechanisms can manipulate players **even without monetization**. — as summarised in search results for the King & Delfabbro debate (primary not retrieved; see [Flinders listing](https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/predatory-monetization-schemes-in-video-games-eg-loot-boxes-and-i/))
- **[Dev — regulation]** China's 2017 law forced Blizzard to publish odds; Korea followed — disclosure is now an industry norm. — [TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/gaming/news/overwatch-loot-box-drop-rates-disclosed-due-to-chinese-law); [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/2407/blizzard-reveals-their-item-rarity-odds-in-korea-to-be-same-as-china)

### Inferences
- Sealed chests opened later on the phone are a good fit: they split one moment into two (the overlay announcement and the private reveal), and both anticipation findings say the gap adds value. Keep the delay short and voluntary — savoring is pleasant, but a reveal gated behind a timer or a currency is the King & Delfabbro "entrapment/pressure" pattern.
- **Avoid even without money**: (a) staged near-misses (e.g. a reel that "almost" lands on legendary) — Larche 2017 shows they drive urge to continue; (b) hidden or misleading odds — publish the rarity table in How to Play, which also matches the "legible" premise; (c) "open more to get the good one" prompts; (d) streak mechanics that reset if you stop playing (sunk-cost pressure). Rarity display should reflect the roll honestly; the reveal can be dramatic without being deceptive.
- Because chests carry no price, the ethical risk is mostly about compulsion and fairness in front of an audience, not money; the Zendle booster-pack result (packs of known structure with guaranteed floors look less gambling-like) argues for **guaranteed floors and visible structure** as the safer shape.

### Gaps
- No study found specifically on delayed (minutes-to-hours) loot reveal vs. immediate reveal in games.
- Xiao et al. 2019 primary text not retrieved.

## 5. Group loot fairness: personal vs. shared loot, participation/casualty rewards

### Takeaway
WoW moved from master/group loot to personal loot (BfA, patch 8.0.1) to remove players' dependence on others' goodwill, then later restored group loot (Dragonflight) because personal loot weakened trading and social bonding; the common thread is that each player wants their own independent roll they can see, plus a reason to stay when things go badly. Participation/consolation rewards for losers are standard practice but I found no rigorous data on their exact sizes.

### Cited Findings
- **[Dev]** BfA (patch 8.0.1) removed Master Loot, leaving Personal Loot; game director Ion Hazzikostas argued master loot left players, especially trial raiders, "at the mercy of others". ### Inferences
- Corrupted Raids already uses the right model for a Twitch chat: **independent personal rolls** (no one can be robbed or "ninja-looted", nobody's reward depends on another viewer), which matches BfA's rationale and suits strangers in chat who have no guild trust. The WoW reversal's lost benefit (trading, social bonding) can be recovered cheaply on the overlay: announcing who found a rare is the social moment, without a shared pool.
- Participation rewards: AGENTS.md already rewards the dead and the defeated (casualty XP x0.4, defeat XP x0.15, plus loot chances). The research supports keeping these, but the *loot* chance for a dead or losing viewer is now 4-8% effective per run, which over a night is small; a pity counter that also ticks on casualty/defeat runs would make "I died, but my next chest is better" legible without inflating drops.
- Tank/healer deaths: roles that die protecting others should not be penalised harder than the DPS who survived because of them; per-member rolls tied only to survival can feel unfair in a shared fight. A flat participation floor (e.g. everyone who joined a WIN gets the same roll, with survival adding only a small bonus) is one option — an inference, not sourced.

### Gaps
- No source quantified consolation/participation reward sizes (e.g. "losers get X% of winners") in shipped games, or measured their effect on retention.
- No data found on Twitch/stream-specific shared reward games (e.g. Marbles on Stream, Stream Raiders) drop rates.
