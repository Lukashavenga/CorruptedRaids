## Q1. Faucet/sink models, MMO inflation, and which sinks work

### Takeaway
The standard model, used in game-design and academic texts and in CCP's published reports, treats currency and items as flows. Faucets create them and sinks destroy them. When faucets outrun sinks for long enough you get inflation, and the remedies with the best record are proportional sinks built into activity players already want to do: transaction taxes, consumables and gambling, upkeep or destruction. Big one-off luxury sinks help with status but do not control inflation.

### Cited Findings
- Lehdonvirta & Castronova, *Virtual Economies: Design and Analysis* (MIT Press, 2014). Chapter 11 introduces "faucets and sinks", "features that add new goods and currency into circulation and features that remove them from circulation", as the main levers of virtual macroeconomics. Research and academic. — [Castronova portfolio](https://edwardcastronova.com/portfolio/virtual-economies-design-and-analysis/); [MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262535069/virtual-economies/); [book text mirror](https://dokumen.pub/virtual-economies-design-and-analysis-information-policy-reprintnbsped-9780262535069-0262535068-0262027259-9780262027250.html)
- Schreiber & Romero, *Game Balance* (CRC Press, 2021). Its "Economic Systems" chapter covers supply, demand, market price and inflation, and splits inflation into **supply-side** and **demand-side** causes. It also has separate sections on F2P and "prestige" economies. Practitioner textbook. — [publisher preview PDF](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781498799584_A38640931/preview-9781498799584_A38640931.pdf); [Schreiber GDC 2016 "A Course About Game Balance" transcript](https://archive.org/stream/GDC2016Schreiber/GDC2016-Schreiber_djvu.txt)
- Joris Dormans' Machinations framework (PhD thesis *Engineering Emergence*, 2012; and Adams & Dormans, *Game Mechanics: Advanced Game Design*) models an economy as sources, pools, converters and drains. It foregrounds feedback loops: positive or negative, constructive or destructive, fast or slow. Its pattern library includes "Converter Engine", "Engine Building" and "Attrition". In one of Dormans' worked examples a converter turns a gold pool into an HP pool and a drain removes HP. Research. — [thesis record, ILLC](https://eprints.illc.uva.nl/id/eprint/2118/); [book TOC](https://designersnotebook.com/Books/Game_Mechanics/game_mechanics.htm); [CWI slides](https://www.cwi.nl/documents/195395/CWI_Scientific_Meeting_rozen_v2.pdf); [AIIDE paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12477)
- **EVE Online (CCP).** CCP publishes a Monthly Economic Report that tracks ISK faucets and sinks, CPI and trade. CCP's own definition: faucets are systems that add ISK, sinks are systems that remove it. — [EVE Vegas 2015 economy update](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/eve-economy-update-eve-vegas-2015-report); [MER Nov 2024](https://www.eveonline.com/de/news/view/monthly-economic-report-november-2024)
- EVE: coverage of a CCP quarterly economic newsletter reported total ISK rising steadily, with the top five sinks removing only about **31% of bounty income**. That is an imbalance a market tax alone could not close. — [Engadget tag page, "isk sink"](https://www.engadget.com/tag/isk-sink)
- EVE, 2021: CCP raised NPC **sales tax from 2.5% to 8%** and the **broker (listing) fee from 2.5% to 3%**, for 11% total. CCP framed this as moving cost "toward the sales tax instead of on the order placement". In other words, charge when value actually changes hands, not for posting. Skills can lower these (Accounting cuts sales tax by 11% per level). — [CCP, "Restructuring Taxes After Relief"](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/restructuring-taxes-after-relief); [EVE University wiki: Trading](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Trading); [Nosy Gamer commentary (opinion)](https://nosygamer.blogspot.com/2021/10/the-tax-holiday-is-over-in-eve-online.html)
- **Old School RuneScape Grand Exchange tax.** Introduced 9 Dec 2021 at **1%**, raised to **2% on 29 May 2025**. Capped at 5M coins per item; items at 50 coins or less are exempt; paid entirely by the seller. Most of the tax is **deleted** (a gold sink). A small share buys specific items from players and deletes a static number of each per week (an "item sink"), stated aims being to control supply, keep old content relevant and prevent item devaluation. — [OSRS Wiki: Grand Exchange](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Grand_Exchange); [OSRS Wiki: Sink (economy)](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Sink_(economy)). Sources disagree on dates for the RS3 version (9 Jan 2023 per [RuneScape Wiki](https://runescape.wiki/w/Grand_Exchange)).
- **Ultima Online.** After the Renaissance expansion added safe non-PvP areas, players could earn money without risk of loss. In response, new "luxury" items were sold at high prices specifically to remove large sums. Secondary source. — [Wikipedia: Gold sink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_sink) (via search summary)
- **WoW's aspirational sink.** The Mighty Caravan Brutosaur sold for **5,000,000 gold** in Battle for Azeroth. It was described as "the ultimate status symbol", and "no matter how bad the gold inflation got… not a sum the vast majority of people possessed". It was removed from vendors at Shadowlands. Blizzard gave no reason; inflation is fan speculation. — [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/world-of-warcraft-caravan-brutosaur/); [warcraftmounts](https://www.warcraftmounts.com/mounts/mightycaravanbrutosaur.php)
- **Albion Online** (player-crafted gear economy): gear loses durability with use, and broken gear needs silver to repair. Full-loot PvP in red/black zones destroys or redistributes gear. Guides describe a continuous "regear" economy in which destruction on death is what keeps crafters' demand alive. Secondary and community sources; they disagree on whether PvE deaths destroy gear. — [Guild Order: Death and Regear Economy](https://guildorder.com/games/albion/wiki/the-death-and-regear-economy); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/761890/discussions/0/1848072002761071092/?l=german); [MassivelyOP (Black Market item sink)](https://massivelyop.com/?p=105879)
- Practitioner framing (opinion): "soft" sinks transfer value between players, "hard" sinks destroy it, and only hard sinks fight inflation. — summarised in [Game Developer, "I designed economies for $150M games"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/i-designed-economies-for-150m-games-here-s-my-ultimate-handbook); [Heroic Labs, why live economies break](https://heroiclabs.com/blog/how-in-game-currencies-work-and-why-live-economies-break/index.html)

### Inferences
- Corrupted Raids' economy today has several faucets (fight gold 5-34, `startingGold` 60, recycle payouts) and essentially one sink, the shop. At `recycleRate: 1` that sink is not even a sink for shop items, because the gold comes back. That matches the existing finding that "gold is not a resource".
- The most transferable sinks for a small game are **proportional, attached to things players want**: a sale tax on auction trades (OSRS 1-2%, EVE about 3.6-11% depending on skills), a gold fee on merging, and consumables. They scale with activity automatically, which a single luxury price does not.
- A cosmetic or status "Brutosaur" item, such as a title, an overlay flourish or a name colour shown on stream, is a good *aspirational* sink for a Twitch game. The audience is literally watching, so status has an unusually large payoff. It should not be relied on for inflation control.
- Item *destruction* (Albion, OSRS's item sink, Kanai's extraction, merging) is what keeps found gear valuable over time. Without an item sink, every drop permanently adds supply.

- No CCP figure found for long-run ISK inflation rates, and the August 2026 MER's sink/faucet numbers were not retrieved.

## Q2. Auction houses: the Diablo III postmortem, PoE's friction, other player markets, and a minimal viable design

### Takeaway
Diablo III is the canonical warning. An efficient, global auction house made buying the dominant way to gear up, which emptied drops of meaning. Blizzard removed both auction houses in 2014 and paired the removal with fewer but better, more targeted drops. Path of Exile keeps trade but deliberately adds friction. The lesson for a loot game is that an auction house should be a *side channel* that is slower, taxed and narrower than playing, and never the shortest path to power.

### Cited Findings
- At GDC 2013, Jay Wilson (former D3 director) said both the real-money and gold auction houses "**really hurt the game**". He said the **gold** AH did more damage, because more players used it and its prices fluctuated more. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2013-03-28-diablo-iiis-auction-house-really-hurt-the-game.html); [MCV/DEVELOP](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/gdc-13-diablo-iii-auction-houses-hurt-game-admits-director/); [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/diablo-3-auction-house-jay-wilson)
- Wilson: "over **50 percent**" of players used the auction houses regularly. Blizzard had expected low uptake and was "completely wrong". He also said "I think we would turn it off if we could." — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/diablo/diablo-3-director-jay-wilson-auction-house-would-turn-it-if-we-could); [MCV/DEVELOP](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/gdc-13-diablo-iii-auction-houses-hurt-game-admits-director/)
- Press summaries of the talk say the AH "damaged item rewards": players stopped playing to kill Diablo and farmed gold and items to trade, so "the auction house became the endgame". — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2013-03-28-diablo-3-director-jay-wilson-auction-houses-really-hurt-game.html); [Ubergizmo](https://www.ubergizmo.com/2013/03/diablo-3s-auction-house-hurt-the-game-according-to-former-game-director-jay-wilson/)
- Removal: announced September 2013, both AHs shut **18 March 2014**, alongside "Loot 2.0". Production director John Hight said the AH "ultimately undermines Diablo's core game play". Loot 2.0 meant **fewer but higher-quality drops, more often suited to your character**. One outlet: "it did feel a little anticlimactic to kill a demon lord, sell his loot to a vendor and then buy optimized gear through the auction house." — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2013/09/18/blizzard-makes-plans-to-remove-all-auction-houses-from-diablo-iii/); [GameBanshee](https://gamebanshee.com/kpdww); [Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2014/06/19/diablo-iii-auction-houses-disappear-on-june-24.aspx); [Blizzplanet](https://blizzplanet.substack.com/p/diablo-iii-pc-auction-house-removal-announced)
- Legal concerns over the real-money AH reportedly slowed the removal. — [GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/articles/diablo-3-controversial-auction-house-would-have-been-removed-faster-if-not-for-legal-concerns/1100-6508613/)
- **Path of Exile Trade Manifesto** (GGG, official forum). It argues that letting players search and buy without interacting would have "very large-scale economic consequences" that would not be good for regular players. It says heavy traders "perform fewer item upgrades to achieve their final build" (trade compresses progression), and that easy trade widens the gap between players because engagement with trade varies a lot. GGG's China (Tencent) build used a market that intentionally makes searching harder and trading easier, and GGG said it was not bringing that to the international version. Claims that GGG "wants friction" in trade come from players and are paraphrase, not quotes. — [Trade Manifesto (mirror)](https://ru.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/2025870); [GameBanshee summary](https://gamebanshee.com/k43qk); player opinion: [PoE Vault on PoE2 friction](https://www.poe-vault.com/poe2/news/path-of-exile-2-players-say-friction-is-killing-the-fun)
- PoE has since added instant-buyout listings for some trades ("Merchant's Tabs"), which remove the whisper-and-wait step. That is evidence even friction-first designers soften it over time. — [mmojugg guide](https://www.mmojugg.com/news/mastering-asynchronous-trade-in-path-of-exile-327.html); [expcarry guide](https://expcarry.com/poe-1-trading-guide-price-buy-sell-items)
- Chris Wilson (GGG) argues that online RPGs must protect their economies even at a cost in revenue, since progression should come from play. — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Path-of-Exile-co-creator-online-RPGs-must-protect-their-economies-even-if-it-costs-developers-cash.1199206.0.html)
- Working market-tax parameters: OSRS GE takes 2% from the seller, capped, and exempts cheap items ([OSRS Wiki](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Grand_Exchange)). EVE takes a 3% broker fee to list plus 8% sales tax, both skill-reducible, with a 1% broker floor ([CCP](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/restructuring-taxes-after-relief); [ISK Scout guide](https://iskscout.com/blog/eve-market-fees-tax-guide)).

### Inferences
- **What went wrong in D3 was efficiency plus centrality, not trade itself.** A global, instant, searchable market lets every player buy the best-in-slot item someone else found, so the expected value of *your own* drop collapses toward its vendor price. With a 10% loot recycle rate and a one-click AH, Corrupted Raids would recreate this exactly. A found item would be worth either 10% (recycle) or whatever the market pays, and the market would set the price of power.
- Mitigations that fit "simple and legible" (design inference, not sourced practice):
  1. **Buyout-only, fixed duration** (e.g. one stream night). No bidding UI, no sniping.
  2. **Seller-paid sale tax as a hard sink** (5-10%, deleted). OSRS-style seller-only taxation is the most legible version: "you get 90%".
  3. **Small up-front listing fee**, refunded or not, to deter spam listings. EVE moved weight *off* listing fees and onto the sale. Keep the listing fee tiny.
  4. **Price floor at the item's recycle value**, so nothing lists below what the system would pay. Optionally add a ceiling (e.g. 2x shop `value`) so a whale cannot price-lock the chat.
  5. **Found gear only** (already planned in #16). Shop items are infinitely supplied and would just arbitrage the shelf.
  6. **Keep the gating rule.** `requires` already gates on allocated points, so the AH cannot make a level-5 character wear endgame gear. That is a natural brake that D3 lacked (D3 gated on level, and power was mostly in gear).
- Friction does not have to be tedium. For a Twitch game, "listings only open at the end of a run" or "N listings per viewer" are readable frictions that also make good on-stream beats.

- Could not read GGG's manifesto or Blizzard's removal post in full, so exact quotes are unverified.
- D3's gold-AH transaction fee (and the RMAH's flat or percentage fee) was not confirmed in the results.
- No sourced data on Diablo II trading (its unofficial "SoJ/High Rune" currency) or on Albion's market tax rates.

## Q3. Merging and upgrade systems: recipes, ratios and their effect on value

### Takeaway
Merge recipes double as **item sinks**. They destroy N items to create 1, which protects the value of drops by giving duplicates a use. Long-lived examples use **3:1** at the low and common end and shift to **2:1** where items are scarce (Diablo II runes), or add a second, scarcer catalyst (a gem, Death's Breath, gold). Ratios should be set against the *value ladder*: if a merge's inputs are worth more than its output, it is a sink; if less, it is a faucet that prints value.

### Cited Findings
- **Diablo II Horadric Cube:** 3 identical gems make 1 gem of the next quality, Chipped through Perfect, at every tier (same for skulls). Low runes (El through Thul) use **3 runes + 1 gem**. From about **Um upward the ratio drops to 2 runes + 1 gem** (e.g. 2 Jah + Flawless Ruby make Cham). Sources disagree on whether Lum through Pul are 3:1 or 2:1; d2r.org reads the game's cubemain.txt. — [Maxroll](https://maxroll.gg/d2/resources/horadric-cube-recipes); [PureDiablo](https://www.purediablo.com/diablo-2/horadric-cube-recipes); [PC Gamer list](https://www.pcgamer.com/diablo-2-horadric-cube-recipes-list/); [d2r.org (157 recipes from cubemain.txt)](https://d2r.org/cube)
- **Diablo III Kanai's Cube** (Reaper of Souls era): extracting a legendary power **destroys the item** (likened to salvaging), and the power goes into an account-wide library. The player can have one weapon, one armour and one jewellery power active. The Cube also upgrades rares to legendaries using Death's Breath. Blood Shards are a single-purpose currency spent only on Kadala's gamble (e.g. armour 25, weapons 75, amulets 100 shards; about 10% legendary/set chance per one 2015-era source). — [Icy Veins Kanai's Cube guide](https://www.icy-veins.com/d3/kanais-cube-guide); [Blizzard forums](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/d3/t/blood-shard-usefulness/532); [Diablo Wiki: Kadala](https://diablo.fandom.com/wiki/Kadala); [Blizzard Watch on drop odds](https://blizzardwatch.com/2015/10/17/diablos-legendary-drop-probabilities-kadala-kanai)
- **Path of Exile vendor recipes:** e.g. a gem at 20% quality, or gems totalling 40% quality, vendor for a Gemcutter's Prism. A full set of level 60-74 rares vendors for Chaos Orbs, with more for unidentified items at 20% quality. These turn "junk" into currency at fixed, published ratios. Secondary guides; details vary. — [Mobalytics](https://mobalytics.gg/poe/guides/vendor-recipes-new-players); [PoE fandom wiki](https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Vendor_recipe_system?oldid=6982)
- **Clash Royale-style duplicate merging:** Supercell publishes per-rarity "copies required to upgrade" tables plus a gold cost per level. Dupes are consumed with gold, so each upgrade is both an item sink and a gold sink. The exact numbers could not be retrieved. — [Supercell blog](https://supercell.com/en/games/clashroyale/blog/News/how-to-get-elite-levels-level-15-2); [RoyaleAPI](https://royaleapi.com/blog/elite-levels-2023-q2-update)

### Inferences
- **Corrupted Raids' value ladder** steps up by about 2.25-2.44x per rarity (20, 45 (2.25x), 110 (2.44x), 260 (2.36x), 600 (2.31x)). So:
  - **3:1** destroys value nominally. Three commons (60 value) make one uncommon (45), losing 25%. Three epics (780) make one legendary (600), losing 23%. It is a healthy **sink**, it feels generous (you get the next tier for sure), and the 3 reads cleanly as "a set".
  - **2:1** *creates* value (40 to 45, 520 to 600). It is a faucet that bypasses drop rarity. Diablo II only uses 2:1 where inputs are already rare and a gem catalyst is also consumed.
  - **5:1** would make a merge feel like a tax rather than a reward when drops run about 0.08-0.15 per viewer per run (AGENTS.md §4). Five commons is about a month of drops for one viewer. Too slow to read as rewarding.
- Recommended shape: **3 same-slot (or same-rarity) items + a gold fee gives 1 random item of the next rarity**. The gold fee is a proportional hard sink, for example about 25-50% of the output's `value`, so roughly 10-20g for common to uncommon and about 150-300g for epic to legendary. That makes gold matter at exactly the moment a player is excited. The fee and the 3:1 together delete both items and gold.
- Merge output should be tagged `source: "loot"` (recycles at 10%). Otherwise merge-then-recycle becomes a gold printer. Also check the arbitrage: 3 found commons recycle for 3 x 2g = 6g, while the merged uncommon recycles for 4.5g, so there is no exploit at 10%. With bought inputs at `recycleRate: 1` (3 x 20 = 60g in, 4.5g out) the merge is a loss, which is fine. **Disallow shop-sourced inputs** anyway, so the shelf cannot be laundered into "found" rarity.
- Merging and the auction house should come in this order: **merge first, AH second**. Merging gives every duplicate drop a use without exposing drop value to market pricing. The AH then trades only what survives that filter.

### Gaps
- Clash Royale's exact copies-per-level and gold tables were not retrieved (the snippet was truncated).
- No sourced player-research on how merge ratios affect *perceived* value. The 3:1 vs 5:1 argument above is inference from drop rates.

## Q4. Salvage and recycle value: what fraction should selling return, and does a low rate make drops feel worthless?

### Takeaway
I found no authoritative "correct fraction". What the sources support is structural. A sell-back price below the buy price exists to stop arbitrage and to make destruction a sink. A low cash rate alone does not make drops worthless *if the item has another use* (wear it, merge it, extract it, trade it). D3 and PoE give salvage and junk a material or currency use rather than just gold. A 10% cash floor is defensible only if merging (and later the AH) gives found items a better outlet.

### Cited Findings
- D3: extracting a legendary in Kanai's Cube **destroys** it, "much like salvaging". The item's value is converted into a permanent, account-wide unlock rather than gold. — [Icy Veins](https://www.icy-veins.com/d3/kanais-cube-guide)
- D3 Loot 2.0's fix for "drops feel worthless" was **fewer, better, more character-relevant drops**, not higher sell prices. — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2013/09/18/blizzard-makes-plans-to-remove-all-auction-houses-from-diablo-iii/)
- PoE vendor recipes convert sets of low-value items into currency at fixed ratios, so "vendor trash" has a defined, small, non-zero value. — [Mobalytics](https://mobalytics.gg/poe/guides/vendor-recipes-new-players)
- Real-world analogue (weak evidence, retail rather than games): hobby shops typically pay about 40-60% of market value in cash and 65-75% in store credit. The spread covers resale risk. Store credit pays more because it keeps money in the store, which is similar to paying salvage in a currency that can only be spent in-game on sinks. — [Keystone Games](https://www.keystonegames.net/blogs/keystone-games-community-news-1/the-two-sides-of-the-counter-understanding-card-trade-in-values)
- OSRS's stated reason for an item sink is to "keep old content relevant and prevent item devaluation". Removing supply protects what drops are worth. — [OSRS Wiki: Sink (economy)](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Sink_(economy))

### Inferences
- **`recycleRate: 1` for shop items is the economy's largest leak.** It turns the only sink into a deposit. It is reasonable for alpha (AGENTS.md says so), but it should come down before gold is expected to mean anything. Common practice suggests somewhere in the 25-50% range (unsourced convention). The key constraint is already in the code: the shelf price must stay above the refund.
- **10% on found items** (2g for a common, 60g for a legendary) is low but defensible *as a floor*. The perceived worth of a drop should come from (a) wearing it, (b) merging it, and (c) eventually selling it on the AH. Until #15 ships, a 10% floor with no other outlet does risk making non-upgrade drops feel like nothing.
- A "store credit" variant fits "less is more": recycle could pay in a merge-only material (shards or dust) instead of gold. That separates the gold economy from the item economy and makes recycling feed merging, a D3-style converter in Dormans' terms. It adds a second currency, which cuts against legibility. Weigh it against simply merging items directly.

### Gaps
- No published designer guideline or player study on sell-back fractions in RPGs was found. The search returned only retail trade-in data.
- No primary Blizzard figures on D3 or D4 salvage yields.

## Q5. Making gold meaningful in a small game

### Takeaway
Gold is meaningful when the things worth wanting cost **multiple sessions** of income and the currency has recurring sinks. At about 5-34g per fight, with 60g starting gold and a full refund on shop items, Corrupted Raids' shop is currently reachable in one to three runs. The fixes are price-to-income ratios, recurring proportional sinks (merge fees, AH tax, consumables), and one or two aspirational, visible purchases.

### Cited Findings
- WoW's 5M-gold Brutosaur worked as a long-horizon aspiration and a status symbol precisely because few could afford it, even under inflation. — [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/world-of-warcraft-caravan-brutosaur/)
- Ultima Online deliberately priced "luxury" items high to drain accumulated wealth once safe zones raised income. — [Wikipedia: Gold sink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_sink)
- D3's Blood Shards: a single-purpose currency spent only on a gamble (25-100 shards per roll). Every unit of that currency ends in a sink, and the gamble is itself the fun. — [Diablo Wiki: Kadala](https://diablo.fandom.com/wiki/Kadala); [Blizzard forums](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/d3/t/blood-shard-usefulness/532)
- Clash Royale charges **gold per card upgrade** on top of duplicate cards, so gold is spent alongside item merging every time. — [Supercell blog](https://supercell.com/en/games/clashroyale/blog/News/how-to-get-elite-levels-level-15-2)
- EVE and OSRS tax market sales (EVE 11% total before skills; OSRS 2%). Trading volume funds a sink that grows with the economy. — [CCP](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/restructuring-taxes-after-relief); [OSRS Wiki](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Grand_Exchange)

### Inferences (worked against the game's numbers)
- Income: about 5-34g per fight, and viewers run 5-20 fights a night (AGENTS.md §2). That is roughly 25-680g a night, with a typical night perhaps around 150-250g. A 600g legendary is about 2-4 nights. A 110g rare is under one night. That is why the shop feels "1-3 runs away", especially with 60g starting gold and full refunds.
- A legible target: **common = one fight, rare = one night, epic = a few nights, legendary = a week or more**. Doing that by raising prices risks the shelf feeling stingy. Doing it with sinks (merge fees, AH tax, a cheaper refund) keeps sticker prices and makes the gold go somewhere.
- Recurring sinks, in order of fit with "simple, legible, rewarding":
  1. **Merge fee** (#15). Paid at the moment of excitement, scales with tier.
  2. **AH sale tax** (#16). Seller-paid, shown as "you receive X".
  3. **Consumables** (4 exist). A repeating, small buy each night.
  4. **Lower `recycleRate`** for shop items (e.g. 0.5) once alpha testing no longer needs free trials.
  5. One **aspirational, on-stream status purchase** (cosmetic, a title, or an overlay flourish). Large, optional, visible to the whole chat. A Twitch-specific advantage over solo games.
- Avoid upkeep or repair sinks (WoW/Albion durability). They are taxes on playing, fit poorly with "everyone gets paid" and the 0.4x casualty XP, and add a chore that reads badly on stream.
- Measure after changing: the repo already logs `economy` events (`buy_gear`, `recycle_gear` with payout and source, `open_chest`) to the balance log. `npm run report:balance` gives gold per viewer per night. That plays the role CCP's faucet/sink report plays for EVE, at small scale.

### Gaps
- No sourced benchmark for the "right" number of sessions to save for an item. The "common = one fight ... legendary = a week" ladder is a design proposal, not a finding.
- No sourced study of chat/stream games' economies specifically (e.g. Twitch channel-point games) was found.
