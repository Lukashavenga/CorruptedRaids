## 1. Common XP curve shapes and their trade-offs; time-to-level targets early vs late

### Takeaway
Almost every long-running RPG uses a per-level cost that rises (polynomial or exponential), so early levels come fast and late levels slow down sharply; the only common "flat" designs are ones that deliberately want levels to stay frequent small rewards. Exponential curves concentrate most of the total grind at the very top (RuneScape: level 92 is half of 99; Path of Exile: 99->100 is reported to cost nearly as much as 1->99), which produces long dead stretches near the cap.

### Cited Findings
- RuneScape (OSRS): reaching 99 in a skill takes 13,034,431 XP; level 92 requires 6,517,253 XP, i.e. just over half of 99. The last 7 levels cost as much as the first 92. — [OSRS Wiki, Experience table](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Experience_table); [OSRS Wiki, Exp](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Exp)
- RuneScape's XP per level rises by roughly 10% per level, making it effectively exponential (doubling about every 7 levels). — [OSRS Wiki, Exp](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Exp) (via search summary); [OSRS Wiki, Level](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Level)
- Path of Exile: players report a death penalty of 10% of the current level's XP from level 71 onwards, and that one death at level 88 cost roughly an hour of play. A cited (unverified) wiki quote says level 98 is roughly 50% of the way from 90 to 100, and another source says 99->100 takes almost as much XP as 1->99. Anecdotal; no official GGG figure found. — [PoE forum, "is the xp lost on death a good design?"](https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/3592296/page/2); [PoE forum, Death Penalty Adjustment Discussion](https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/1217342); [timesaver.gg (SEO blog, low weight)](https://timesaver.gg/blog/poe2-how-long-to-reach-level-100)
- An SEO blog claims reaching level 100 in Path of Exile 2 takes most players 150-300+ hours; unverified, low weight. — [timesaver.gg](https://timesaver.gg/blog/poe2-how-long-to-reach-level-100)
- Pokemon uses cubic cumulative curves per species "growth rate": Fast 0.8n^3 (800,000 XP at 100), Medium Fast n^3 (1,000,000), Medium Slow 1.2n^3 - 15n^2 + 100n - 140 (1,059,860), Slow 1.25n^3 (1,250,000); Erratic (600,000) and Fluctuating (1,640,000) are piecewise. A cubic cumulative curve means per-level cost grows ~quadratically. — [Serebii, EXP](https://serebii.net/games/exp.shtml); [PokemonDB Q&A](https://pokemondb.net/pokebase/126233/what-are-all-growth-rates-in-pokemon)
- Design writing: a linear increment asks for the same extra XP each level; an exponential curve makes each level costlier than the last so late levelling slows; a logarithmic curve speeds up the more you play; all are valid depending on the game. — [Davide Aversa, "GameDesign Math: RPG Level-based Progression"](https://davideaversa.it/blog/gamedesign-math-rpg-level-based-progression/) (via search summary)
- Game Developer article compares linear and exponential threshold techniques; summary states a linear-increment curve gives easy early thresholds that then grow without becoming hyperbolic. — [Game Developer, "Quantitative design - How to define XP thresholds"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/quantitative-design---how-to-define-xp-thresholds-) (page could not be fetched; search summary only)
- A practitioner blog suggests a polynomial exponent of ~1.5 for a moderate curve, ~2.0 for a long late game, 2.5+ for MMO-style grinds. Opinion, unvalidated. — [StraySpark, RPG stat systems](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/rpg-stat-systems-character-progression-design)
- Idle/incremental games: costs grow exponentially while production grows more slowly (polynomially) because a player never has infinite generators, so costs eventually outrun income and force a reset/prestige; this is the deliberate source of the "wall". — [Pecorella, "The Math of Idle Games, Part I"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-math-of-idle-games-part-i); [Part II](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/the-math-of-idle-games-part-ii); [GDC Europe 2016 "Quest for Progress" slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/quest-for-progress-gdc-europe-2016/65405507) (via search summaries)

### Inferences
- The current Corrupted Raids curve (+10 XP per level) is already "polynomial" in cumulative terms (~5L^2) even though the per-level step is linear. The problem the streamer describes is not the shape but the *scale*: at level 1-20 a level costs 50-240 XP, which at typical run payouts is about one run per level, so the bar fills constantly and each fill grants only 2 points.
- An exponential curve (RuneScape/PoE style) would be wrong for a shared-bar Twitch game: it pushes most of the total time into the last few levels, creating exactly the long dead stretches the chat will watch. A curve whose per-level time grows to a plateau (e.g. steep early ramp, then near-constant time per level) fits better: early levels in 1-3 runs, mid levels in roughly a night, late levels capped at a fixed number of nights.
- Concrete shape option: define the target *runs per level* rather than XP, e.g. runs(L) = min(cap, a*L^p). With a target of ~60 levels (see Q2), p ~ 1 and a plateau of ~1 stream night per level late on keeps the bar moving visibly every session.

- No authoritative WoW XP-per-level table or official Blizzard time-to-cap targets were retrieved.
- No reliable official figures for Path of Exile time to level 100.

## 2. "Fewer, bigger levels" vs "many small levels"; level squish; horizontal vs vertical; power creep

### Takeaway
The clearest industry precedent is Blizzard cutting level caps because too many levels made each one meaningless: WoW went 120 -> 50 (+10 to 60) in Shadowlands, explicitly so levels would "feel meaningful again", and Diablo IV cut its cap 100 -> 60 and moved further growth into a separate, account-wide Paragon track. Both moved toward fewer, bigger character levels with long-tail progress shifted elsewhere.

### Cited Findings
- WoW, March 2019 developer Q&A (Ion Hazzikostas, summarised): 120 is a very large number of levels; it would be too overwhelming and not fun to reward players for each earned level; the team was internally discussing a level squish to make levels feel meaningful again. — [Icy Veins, Live Developer Q&A roundup, March 21st](https://icy-veins.com/forums/topic/42603-live-developer-qa-roundup-march-21st) (search summary; page fetch blocked); [Blizzard Watch, Mar 2019 "level squish inevitable"](https://blizzardwatch.com/2019/03/22/level-squish-inevitable/); [Mein-MMO, "WoW is considering lowering the max level from 120 to 60"](https://mein-mmo.de/en/wow-is-considering-lowering-the-max-level-from-120-to-60,331652)
- WoW Shadowlands pre-patch squished characters from 120 to 50, with 10 more levels to 60; stated aim was to make levelling faster, more meaningful and more accessible to new players. — [Wikipedia, WoW: Shadowlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_of_Warcraft:_Shadowlands); [DBLTAP pre-patch summary](https://www.dbltap.com/posts/shadowlands-pre-patch-everything-you-need-to-know-01ee16dspdx3)
- Commentary disagrees on whether the squish itself was the fix: Blizzard Watch's editor argued the levelling overhaul fixed most problems and the squish "may not have been strictly necessary"; Massively OP argued the squish alone did not solve the underlying problem. Opinion. — [Blizzard Watch, Sept 2020](https://blizzardwatch.com/2020/09/24/world-warcraft-need-level-squish/); [Massively OP, Apr 2019](https://massivelyop.com/2019/04/12/wow-factor-the-level-squish-isnt-the-whole-solution-for-world-of-warcraft/)
- WoW has also repeatedly squished *item/stat* numbers, and a further "item squish" was reported for the Midnight expansion ("your numbers are going down ... not a bug") - i.e. vertical gear power creep has to be periodically reset. — [Icy Veins, "Your Numbers Are Going Down in Midnight"](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow/news/your-numbers-are-going-down-in-midnight-and-its-not-a-bug/); [Mein-MMO, item squish](https://mein-mmo.de/wow-item-squish-naechste-erweiterung/)
- Diablo IV (Vessel of Hatred / Season 6, Oct 2024): level cap reduced from 100 to 60; after 60, characters earn Paragon points, now account/realm-wide with a cap of 300; existing level-100 characters became level 50 with 200 Paragon points. Blizzard said levelling would not be slowed down. Stated rationale per secondary sources: streamline endgame progression and enhance customisation; no direct Blizzard quote retrieved. — [Maxroll, Season 6 change log](https://maxroll.gg/d4/news/season-6-change-log); [Kotaku, 2.0 PTR patch notes](https://kotaku.com/diablo-4-big-changes-update-2-0-ptr-patch-notes-dlc-pc-1851636919); [WoWCarry blog (secondary)](https://wowcarry.com/blog/diablo-4/new-level-cap-and-paragon-system-introduced-in-diablo-4-vessel-of-hatred-for-season-6)
- Diablo IV originally put level 85 at roughly halfway to 100 by XP, prompting comparisons with RuneScape's 92/99 - evidence players notice and dislike back-loaded curves. — [TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/91936/diablo-iv-gamers-relate-to-runescape-players-after-realizing-level-85-is-halfway-100/) (headline via search result)
- Vertical progression = the gear/level climb; horizontal = more options rather than more power (Guild Wars 2 masteries cited as the main example). Massively OP argues the two are parallel tracks and horizontal alone can be "a lot less rewarding"; a 2025 column says nobody does pure vertical without layering horizontal on top. Opinion/editorial. — [Engadget, "MMO Mechanics: Comparing vertical and horizontal progression"](https://www.engadget.com/2014-02-05-mmo-mechanics-comparing-vertical-and-horizontal-progression.html); [Massively OP, Apr 2025](https://massivelyop.com/2025/04/03/vague-patch-notes-whats-so-hopeless-about-horizontal-progression-in-mmos/); [Massively OP, Jul 2025](https://massivelyop.com/2025/07/03/massively-overthinking-is-vertical-progression-still-sustainable-for-mmos/)
- Torchlight III's developers framed "horizontal progression" as tying power to gear rather than character level, with scaling keeping older gear relevant. — [Torchlight III, "The Vision: Horizontal Progression"](https://www.torchlight3.com/de/news-article/11006473)

### Inferences
- The WoW and Diablo IV precedents line up with the streamer's report: when a level grants a small, uniform reward (here 2 points), many levels dilute each one. The fix both studios chose was fewer levels with something noticeable at each, and an *uncapped/long* secondary track for those who keep playing.
- For Corrupted Raids, a cap of ~50-60 levels (vs 300) with a bigger grant per level (e.g. 8-12 points, or 2 points plus an unlock every 5-10 levels) would give the same total points with a level-up 5-6x rarer and 5-6x bigger. If the year-long goal must stay, a Paragon-like long tail (e.g. a prestige counter or cosmetic rank after cap) carries it, rather than 240 more identical levels.
- Because gear is what players love, "bigger levels" need not mean bigger stat grants: the level-up could instead raise what gear can drop for you or unlock a slot/feature, so the level feeds the gear loop instead of competing with it.

### Gaps
- No primary Blizzard blog quote for Diablo IV's rationale for the 100->60 change was retrieved (devtrackers.gg fetch blocked).
- No quantitative data (retention, engagement) comparing few-big vs many-small levels was found; the evidence is developer statements and editorials.

## 3. Level vs gear as the progression axis; gating gear by level vs stats vs not at all

### Takeaway
Gear-as-progression systems (Destiny Power, WoW item level, Diablo III Loot 2.0) make the character's number the average of what they wear, which makes every drop potentially a "level-up" - but they need explicit caps/soft-caps to stop the climb becoming an endless chore, and Loot 2.0 shows the drop rate should be lower with each drop more often useful. Stat requirements on gear (Elden Ring/Soulslike) tie gear to build commitment; they are evidence of a working design pattern but players mainly experience them as "spend just enough to wield it".

### Cited Findings
- Destiny 2 Power is the average level of equipped gear; below the "soft cap" ordinary drops usually exceed your average, so early progress is fast; past it only weekly "Powerful" rewards and "Pinnacle" rewards from the hardest content raise Power. Example caps: Lightfall/Season of the Wish soft 1,750, powerful 1,800, pinnacle 1,810; caps rise every season/expansion. — [GGRecon, Lightfall power cap](https://www.ggrecon.com/guides/destiny-2-power-cap-reach-quickly); [Gfinity, Season 13 caps](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/destiny-2-season-13-season-of-the-chosen-soft-hard-pinnacle-power-cap-confirmed-ps4-xbox-pc); [High Ground Gaming, power level guide](https://www.highgroundgaming.com/destiny-2-power-level-guide/)
- Destiny 2 Edge of Fate (2025) reset everyone to Power 10 with a cap of 200 (seasonal 450) and refocused on "a deeper pursuit of Power and ascending to higher Tiers"; Bungie later admitted the update "pushed the game in the wrong direction" and that the Power/Tier grind "will never be a substitute for earning a trophy"; launch had one of the franchise's lowest player counts. — [The Game Post, "Bungie: Edge of Fate was the wrong path"](https://thegamepost.com/bungie-edge-of-fate-was-the-wrong-path-destiny-2-power-tier-grind/); [Wikipedia, Destiny 2: The Edge of Fate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destiny_2:_The_Edge_of_Fate); [Gfinity, launch player counts](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/destiny-2s-edge-of-fate-launches-with-one-of-the-lowest-player-counts-in-franchise-history)
- Diablo III Loot 2.0 (patch 2.0.1, Feb 2014): fewer items drop but with better stats ("quantity for quality"); "Smart Drops" bias rare/legendary stats toward the player's class; stat ranges narrowed so a drop is more meaningful. Kevin Martens (BlizzCon) described it as better, smarter drops more frequently so players need to hold onto items less. Secondary/wiki sources. — [Diablo Wiki, Loot 2.0](https://di.diablowiki.net/Loot_2.0); [Diablo Fandom, Loot 2.0](https://diablo.fandom.com/wiki/Loot_2.0); [Shacknews, patch 2.0.1](https://www.shacknews.com/article/83266/diablo-3-patch-201-deploys-with-loot-20); [GameBanshee BlizzCon Q&A](https://gamebanshee.com/68); [Game Informer, five reasons to return](https://www.gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2014/02/28/five-reasons-to-return-to-diablo-after-patch-2-0-1.aspx)
- Loot 2.0 was later criticised for working "too well": players completed builds early in a season and got bored, which prompted Ancient items as a further vertical tier. Forum opinion. — [Blizzard forums, "How much randomness is too much?"](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/d3/t/d4-how-much-randomness-is-too-much/49525)
- Elden Ring: weapons have Strength/Dexterity/etc. requirements; common guidance is to put only enough points into STR/DEX to meet the requirements of the weapon you want unless committing to a build; shortfalls can be patched with talismans/buffs. Guide-level evidence. — [Game8, Elden Ring stats](https://game8.co/games/Elden-Ring/archives/457799); [GameWith, Elden Ring](https://gamewith.net/elden-ring/article/show/33174)

### Inferences
- Corrupted Raids already gates gear on attribute points spent (like Elden Ring), not level. That is defensible (it makes allocation a build decision), but combined with tiny levels it means a player's gear access creeps up 2 points at a time; each level silently unlocks nothing visible. Making levels rarer and bigger would make each level-up cross gear thresholds more often, so the level-up and the gear loop reinforce each other.
- A Destiny-style "Corruption = average of equipped gear power" already exists in spirit (Corruption is derived from attributes + gear). If gear is to be THE axis, the visible headline number should be gear-driven (item power / Corruption), with Level demoted to a secondary meta-track. The Destiny lessons: (a) a soft cap where routine drops stop helping and only rarer sources push higher keeps late gear meaningful; (b) a hard reset of that number is badly received.
- The Loot 2.0 lesson matches the repo's 2026-10-26 decision to lower drop rates: fewer drops are fine only if a larger share are upgrades/class-appropriate. Smart-loot weighting toward the player's role (Tank/Healer/DPS stats) would be the low-complexity equivalent.
- "Less is more": the simplest model consistent with all three precedents is two numbers - Gear Power (main, climbs through drops) and Level (rare, each one a visible event that unlocks something gear-related).

### Gaps
- No primary Blizzard or Bungie design postmortem was retrieved (fetches blocked/none found); rationales are from interviews, patch coverage, and wikis.
- No source found directly comparing level-gated vs stat-gated vs ungated gear in terms of player satisfaction; Diablo 2 attribute requirements search returned nothing useful.
- No WoW item-level design rationale document was retrieved beyond the squish coverage.

## 4. Progression legibility and motivation: goal-gradient, endowed progress, SDT, shared progress bars

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed evidence supports three things relevant here: people accelerate effort as they near a visible goal (goal-gradient), giving a head start on the bar raises completion (endowed progress), and perceived competence/autonomy predict game enjoyment and continued play (SDT). All three argue for progress bars that are short enough to visibly approach completion within a session and reset to a fresh, slightly pre-filled goal.

### Cited Findings
- Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng (2006), "The Goal-Gradient Hypothesis Resurrected", Journal of Marketing Research 43(1):39-58 (peer-reviewed). Coffee-card field study: members bought coffee more frequently the closer they were to a free coffee; inter-purchase time fell by about 20% from the first to the last stamp. Customers given a 12-stamp card with 2 pre-filled "bonus" stamps completed the 10 required purchases faster than those given a blank 10-stamp card (illusionary goal progress). After redeeming, acceleration restarted on the next card ("reset"). — [Columbia Business School PDF](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/1200/goalgradient.pdf) (fetch blocked; figures from search summaries); [Columbia Insights summary](https://business.columbia.edu/insights/chazen-global-insights/goal-gradient-hypothesis-resurrected-purchase-acceleration); [Chicago Booth Review](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/going-goal)
- Nunes & Dreze (2006), "The Endowed Progress Effect", Journal of Consumer Research 32(4):504-512 (peer-reviewed). Car-wash field experiment: 300 cards; an 8-stamp empty card vs a 10-stamp card with 2 stamps pre-applied (same 8 paid washes). Reported completion: 34% (endowed) vs 19% (blank), with the endowed group also finishing faster. Effect stronger when a reason for the head start is given. Figures from secondary summaries; one summary's "82%" figure is the relative uplift (34/19) and is not a separate result. — [Wharton Knowledge, loyalty programs](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/podcast/knowledge-at-wharton-podcast/the-lowdown-on-customer-loyalty-programs-which-are-the-most-effective-and-why/); [Coglode summary](https://coglode.com/nuggets/endowed-progress-effect); [Silicon Canals summary](https://siliconcanals.com/t-car-wash-loyalty-cards-endowed-progress/)
- Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski (2006), "The Motivational Pull of Video Games: A Self-Determination Theory Approach", Motivation and Emotion 30:347-363, doi:10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8 (peer-reviewed). Four studies (players trying 1, 2 and 4 games; plus a survey of an MMO community) found perceived in-game autonomy and competence predicted enjoyment, game preference and changes in well-being; relatedness also predicted outcomes in the multiplayer study; intuitive controls and presence related to competence/autonomy. Led to the PENS measure. — [University of Rochester PDF](https://www.rochester.edu/warner/lida/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/02bfe513dd59366750000000.pdf); [ScienceDaily, Dec 2006](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2006/12/061226134706.htm); [SDT.org, PENS](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/player-experience-of-needs-satisfaction-pens/)

### Inferences
- Goal-gradient implies effort/attention peaks near the end of a bar. A bar that refills every run gives no "near the end" phase to accelerate into; a bar sized to fill once per one-to-two streams gives chat a visible run-up ("two more runs to level") - the moment of highest engagement.
- Endowed progress suggests that after a level-up the next bar should start visibly part-filled (e.g. the level-up grants 10-20% of the next level, or overflow XP carries over and is shown), and that framing matters ("bonus for showing up tonight").
- SDT competence is better served by gear that measurably changes the fight (the repo's own §6 finding: gear moves difficulty more than any enemy stat) than by +2 attribute points; autonomy is served by the attribute-gated gear choice. Relatedness is the shared-chat aspect: a shared bar or party milestone (e.g. "chat has cleared X runs tonight") is a natural relatedness lever.
- These marketing studies are about loyalty cards, not games; transfer to game XP bars is an inference, though a widely made one.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found specifically on *shared/social* progress bars (whole audience watching one bar) or on Twitch viewer-engagement game progression.
- Exact figures for both loyalty-card studies could not be verified against the original PDFs (fetch blocked).

## 5. Concrete pacing guidance for short-session / session-based games (10-60 min)

### Takeaway
Little hard, published pacing guidance with numbers was found; the best-documented framework (idle-game math) is about deliberately letting costs outgrow income to create walls and then resetting via prestige. For a short-session, shared-viewing game, the transferable principles are: guarantee a visible step every session, put the big rewards on a slower track, and avoid back-loaded curves.

### Cited Findings
- Idle games balance production rates against costs; early in a run production outpaces costs, then costs become prohibitive, creating a wall that prestige/reset mechanics address. Exponential cost growth outpaces polynomial production growth. — [Pecorella, Math of Idle Games Part I](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-math-of-idle-games-part-i); [Part II](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/the-math-of-idle-games-part-ii); [Part III](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-math-of-idle-games-part-iii); [Wikipedia, Incremental game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incremental_game)
- Diablo IV's response to a long, back-loaded level climb was to cut base levels to 60 (said not to slow levelling) and move long-tail growth into account-wide Paragon (cap 300). — [Maxroll, Season 6 change log](https://maxroll.gg/d4/news/season-6-change-log); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/diablo-4-big-changes-update-2-0-ptr-patch-notes-dlc-pc-1851636919)
- Destiny's weekly Powerful/Pinnacle rewards structure late progression as a fixed number of meaningful steps per week (time-gated), rather than an open-ended grind. — [PC Gamer, Destiny 2 levelling guide](https://www.pcgamer.com/destiny-2-beyond-light-leveling-guide-power-fast); [High Ground Gaming](https://www.highgroundgaming.com/destiny-2-power-level-guide/)

### Inferences (worked numbers for Corrupted Raids; researcher's calculations, not sourced)
- Current state, from the repo's formula: level 1->10 costs 810 XP total and per-level cost only reaches ~1,000 XP around level 96. At the AGENTS.md figure of ~158 XP per run at full rate (2,920 runs to 300), a level in the first 20 takes ~0.3-1.5 runs - i.e. multiple level-ups per session, each worth 2 points. The 25% `xpRate` multiplies run counts by 4 but leaves the shape: many tiny levels early, then a long linear slog (level 299->300 = 3,030 XP, ~19 full-rate runs).
- A "fewer, bigger levels" target that fits a 5-20 runs/day, 5 days/week cadence (roughly 25-100 runs/week):
  - Levels 1-5: one level per ~2-4 runs (first stream gives 1-3 level-ups - an onboarding reward).
  - Levels 6-30: one level per ~1 stream (~10-15 runs), so every stream the chat watches bars approach and cross once.
  - Levels 31-60: plateau at ~1 level per 2-3 streams (~25-40 runs), never longer, so no dead stretch exceeds about a week.
  - Total ~60 levels in roughly 1,500-2,000 runs (~4-8 months at mid cadence); a post-cap meta-track (prestige rank / cosmetic) carries the "year" goal for the most active.
  - Per-level grant scaled so total points are preserved: 300 levels x 2 = 600 points -> 60 levels x 10 points, or fewer points per level plus a per-level unlock (gear tier visible in the shop, a new loot table, a cosmetic).
- One simple way to express that as a single formula: runs_per_level(L) = min(R_max, R_0 + k*L), e.g. R_0 = 2, k = 0.4, R_max = 35, then XP_to_next(L) = runs_per_level(L) x average XP per run. This is still "linear then flat", stays legible, and avoids the RuneScape/PoE back-loading.
- Gear should be the main within-session reward: with drops now ~0.08-0.15 items per viewer per run (AGENTS.md §4), a typical viewer doing 10-15 runs finds about one item per night; if gear is the main axis, that item needs a high chance of being an upgrade (Loot 2.0 / smart-loot logic), or a pity/bad-luck counter so no viewer goes several nights with nothing.

### Gaps
- No published numeric guideline (GDC talk, postmortem, or paper) giving target minutes/sessions per level for 10-60 minute session games was found; the numbers above are derived, not sourced.
- Schreiber & Romero "Game Balance" and Dormans/Adams "Game Mechanics: Advanced Game Design" were not retrieved; their treatment of progression curves is not represented here.
- No data on Twitch-integrated games specifically (e.g. Stream Raiders, Marbles on Stream) progression pacing was retrieved.
